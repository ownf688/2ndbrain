## Key discussion points

### Background and regional connection

Dom has an unusual and valuable profile for this role. Born in France to Central African parents, raised in Cameroon for 15 years, then 10 years in Canada, masters in London, 4 years running commercial operations at a microfinance company in Central African Republic, then MBA at HEC Paris. His capstone was on alternative data for SME financial services in Africa. He discovered NALA through his research on Ghana.

> [!quote]- Source
> "I was really interested into fintech to know how, you know, these solutions could actually help bridge the gap between the unbanked and the financial services"

### Grassroots growth scenario

Ryan ran a scenario: event in 3 weeks, no social media, taxi + phone + WhatsApp, get 50-100 people. Dom gave a specific, credible answer: go to Barbes (immigrant neighborhood in Paris with heavy Cameroonian/Senegalese/Ivorian presence), work shop-by-shop, leverage a cultural space run by young guys, use WhatsApp networks. Importantly, he'd pick one neighborhood and go deep rather than spreading thin. He also drew the distinction between entertainment ambassadors (like TapTapSend's TV sponsorships) and cultural/community ambassadors who carry more trust.

> [!quote]- Source
> "I would definitely go to Barbes... a lot of saloons, a lot of markets, a lot of different like local shops are owned by Africans in general"

### Activation diagnostics

Ryan followed up: 35 new signups from the event, zero first transfers. Dom identified 4 possible causes: (1) poor onboarding / no hands-on demo, (2) app errors, (3) UX not compelling enough for return usage, (4) people only wanted the incentive. Then he correctly identified that the right response is to reach out directly rather than guess. Good diagnostic thinking.

> [!quote]- Source
> "the best way to find out would be actually to reach out"

### TapTapSend competitive awareness

Dom showed awareness of the competitive landscape. Noted TapTapSend is a major competitor in France, funding TV shows and using entertainment ambassadors. His view: NALA should differentiate by going with more "serious" cultural ambassadors who carry organic trust rather than paid entertainment promotion. Also noted NALA's rate comparison feature is unique, no competitors offer it.

> [!quote]- Source
> "TapTapSend is one of the biggest competitors of NALA... I think NALA should go through a different route, which is more of like cultural kind of ambassadors"

### Role structure and expectations

Ryan explained the role structure: in-country teams (Country Manager + Partnerships Lead) report into the Growth Manager. The GM reports to Imad (P&L lead). GM expected to travel frequently to sending markets (US, UK, Europe) for grassroots activation. Critical distinction: not just a community events person, but someone who can run analytical reports on event performance and feed insights to product.

> [!quote]- Source
> "ideally we'd love for someone who's like comfortable going to a church event or a food market event on a Sunday, but then on the Monday is running an analytical report"
