## Key Discussion Points

### Career trajectory: law to regulator to builder

Karanjit trained as a lawyer but chose financial services. Moved from Financial Ombudsman to a small investment bank, then KPMG (2.5 years, Big Four consulting across Barclays, Credit Suisse, UBS, Goldman Sachs), then RBS group compliance, then FCA for 10 years. He completed what he calls "the holy trinity" of consulting, banking, and regulator experience. After the FCA, he deliberately chose the riskiest option -- CRO at Viva, a payments acquirer -- because he questioned his own skill set after years of senior meetings.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> "When I left, I almost questioned my discernible skill set. I'm like, what do I do other than attend meetings and speak to heads of business? So I took the most risky, the most unsure role that I was offered, which was the CRO role at Viva to go in and build the business."

### Building compliance from zero at Viva

Joined Viva with no UK licence, no team, no London office. Won the FCA licence by personally training and coaching C-suite through 6-7 rounds of regulatory interviews. Signed a London office lease himself and hired 20 people. Built all policies, processes, risk frameworks from scratch. During his tenure, JP Morgan took a 49% stake (1 billion euros) and the business grew 30% year-on-year. Has been there 3.5 years, the employer is trying to retain him, and he manages 14-15 people.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> "When I joined, there was no license. We had no license in the UK, so I helped them win the license with interviewing with the regulators. So I went through 6 or 7 rounds of interviews. I trained all the other C-suite people because, in the nicest possible way, they weren't as seasoned as I was."

### AI adoption in compliance

Already using Claude for: (1) a live risk register that replaces enterprise software costing 150k/year, (2) automated regulatory alert triage from EMA trade body emails, scoring each item by business impact, (3) API integrations with Power BI and Jira via Claude. Not technical -- describes himself as "super not technologically advanced" -- but strong at prompting and directing engineers to build API interfaces. Has given his whole team Claude memberships and has them working in shared projects.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> "If you think you're replicating enterprise-level software that you're paying 150 grand a year for, that's pretty good. It's pretty good. That's 150,000 you can put towards Claude."

### Commercial pragmatism on regulatory trade-offs

When Peter presented the 30-day vs 90-day notification scenario, Karanjit gave a textbook commercially-aware answer: present the risks, benchmark against competitors, frame within board risk appetite, let the business decide. But he added a critical nuance Peter hadn't considered -- the "totting up" risk, where multiple small regulatory shortcuts compound into a pattern that triggers scrutiny. He draws a hard line only at personal criminal liability, not as a reflex blocker.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> "Sometimes it can be a totting up thing. You know, the regulators will be watching from afar and going, okay, they've done this thing... We probably need to rein it in a bit now because you don't want the regulatory scrutiny."

### Risk register approach for NALA

When asked about first 90-180 day approach, outlined: (1) audit what exists and what systems are in use, (2) validate accuracy before migrating, (3) centralise into a document management system, (4) migrate sanitised data to Claude for a live, dynamic risk register, (5) set up dissemination to risk owners. Referenced COSO international standards as the benchmark. Acknowledged he'd be "coming from a position of blindness" and would need to understand NALA's specific setup first.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> "Is it something that's recognized by COSO, you know, international risk management standards? Does it look right? Does it feel right? Is it right with the business owners? Pull that over, get it sanitized, and then work from there with a fresh base, a clean kind of back book."
