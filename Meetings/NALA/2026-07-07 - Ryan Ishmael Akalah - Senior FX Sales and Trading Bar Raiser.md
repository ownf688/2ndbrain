## Key discussion points

### Trading philosophy - relationships over execution

Ryan's core thesis: "what distinguishes you from every other trader is having a personal relationship with customers." Grew customer volumes $1k to $15M through relationship building at Flutterwave. Customers followed him to Klasha by name. This directly matches Peter's stated need for NALA to treat FX counterparties as customers, not arm's-length transaction endpoints.

> [!quote]- Source
> "what distinguishes you from every other trader is having a personal relationship with customers"

### Klasha desk build - 360-degree operational experience

Joined Klasha when customers had disappeared. Built FX desk infrastructure from ground up: banking setup (critical - without banking, FX is impossible and you're exposed to third-party customer poaching), KYC for LPs and customers, crypto exchange platform integrations for settlement. Took ownership far beyond the trading role.

> [!quote]- Source
> "if a company doesn't have banking and they want to do FX, it's literally impossible"

### Peter's NALA vision (business intel)

B2B book: "best 3 months ever, just like by far." Shifting from consumer-fueling to dual approach: (1) bread-and-butter traders maintaining predictable local currency rates for consumer payouts, (2) opportunity-focused traders doing agile back-to-back trades for B2B (e.g. MoneyGram needs $1M Ugandan shillings). "Ari and the team" run trading, report to Peter. Peter took over B2B sales ~3 months ago. FX counterparties need to be treated as customers - deeper relationships needed, especially in East Africa corridor.

> [!quote]- Source
> "I don't think we've been treating the FX counterparties as if they are also customers"

### OTC vs API nuance

Ryan's take: APIs for scale (can't trade $1,000 x 16 all day, need $5M trades), but OTC relationships are what bring institutional customers. Customers know who sets API rates. Decision-making and human touch distinguish companies. Sophisticated answer that shows market maturity.

> [!quote]- Source
> "decision-making and the real touch with a customer, what really brings a customer to the company, is going to be OTC trading"

### Process and infrastructure building

At Flutterwave: initiated daily WhatsApp outreach to counterparties (not existing when he arrived). Identified revenue tracking gap - cross-border vs remittance transaction splitting wasn't tracked, meaning revenue was being underreported. At Klasha: set up end-to-end banking and KYC infrastructure.

### Platform experience

Bloomberg, Refinitiv, Citi Velocity, SBSA. Conversant with all.
