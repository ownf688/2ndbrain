Lynnette presented a restructuring plan for 3 Senegal-based CS agents. Automation (Kiara) has absorbed significant CS volume; the actual human workload has shrunk. Josh wants to right-size. India CS has also seen 2 voluntary departures.

Josh makes that call directly — no need to loop Frank (FinCrime), which reduces leak risk. The other two CS agents are harder to redeploy; their skills are CS/ops-native. **Phase 2 — Separation:** statutory notice (1 month) + Senegal severance (25% of monthly salary per year of service, lower than Kenya's 50%) + additional economic settlement month from Josh. Total exceeds statutory minimum. The word "redundancy" is avoided; approach as separation of contract.

**Timeline concern:** Owen pushed back on the 2-month end-to-end plan. Once employees sense what's coming, motivation collapses and resentment builds. Lynnette clarified the background work (redeployment exploration, documentation) runs without employee awareness — employee-facing window is only 1-2 weeks. She will compress.

**"Choice model":** Owen proposed offering a financial incentive for mutual separation (better terms than statutory termination), giving employees agency and potentially speeding resolution. Lynnette flagged that Senegal courts have used "without prejudice" conversations as evidence even when framed as protected — she will research. The structure proposed: two options with different financial outcomes — which do you choose?

**Misclassification risk:** Owen raised that contractors in role 1+ year can be treated as employees under Senegal law, exposing NALA to back-tax liability on top of separation packages. If contested and lost: 12 months damages + misclassification exposure. Lynnette to add a cost-exposure slide.
