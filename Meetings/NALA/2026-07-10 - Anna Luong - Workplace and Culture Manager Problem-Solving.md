## Key Discussion Points

### 1. Career background and motivation

Anna has 5+ years in office management, spanning retail origins, architecture firms, a 4-year stint at insurtech startup Superscript (~50 people), and 7 months at Airwallex (~2,000 people, EMEA workplace team). She is leaving Airwallex because the role is too narrow -- focused only on social/culture, not broader ops/facilities.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> "I quite missed the broader role that I had and being able to support the people, the operations, facilities side... I know that I have a lot more to give, but because we're so big here, right, people function is broken down to workplace team, which is what I sit in, the people team, and then the talent team."

### 2. Proudest projects

Two examples offered: (a) organising a Christmas party in her first 2 months at Superscript that "people still talked about a few years later," and (b) migrating 120 laptops from Fleetsmith to DataJar when the head of tech relocated to Australia.

The Christmas party story demonstrates initiative and execution under time pressure but is fundamentally an event-logistics achievement, not a culture-shaping one. The IT migration shows willingness to step outside comfort zone and composure under pressure.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> "How are you staying so calm in this, Anna? Like, I'm panicking. And I was like, well, if I panic, you're gonna panic as well."

### 3. C-level approval delay problem (key problem-solving question)

When asked how she handles C-level delays on event approvals, Anna's approach was: (1) find out the venue's absolute last deadline, (2) ask for an extension, (3) go through the EA as intermediary, (4) present full background with a backup option.

This is a reasonable but shallow answer. No attempt to categorise the problem (is it an information problem? a prioritisation problem? a decision-authority problem?). No mention of pre-empting by batching decisions, setting standing authority thresholds, or creating decision templates. The backup-venue idea is practical but reactive.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> "Perhaps I would go to you first and say, Susanna, you know, this is the last date that this venue is giving us... And maybe even have a backup as well, that if they can't make that decision by like the 30th of June, I'll say, okay, this is like a second option."

### 4. Office behaviour management (dishwasher problem)

When asked how she improved basic office hygiene behaviour, the toolkit was: Slack reminders, onboarding hints, leveraging a teammate who made memes, and casual in-person nudges.

No measurement, no escalation framework, no behavioural design thinking (e.g., removing friction, changing defaults, gamification with tracking). The meme idea was someone else's contribution, not hers.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> "Our main tool of communication was Slack, so I honestly would send out like Slack from my whole Slack team reminders... he was just like the fun joker guy. I'd be like, can you help me just send out a meme?"

### 5. Culture leadership approach

When asked how she would lead culture at NALA, the answer centred on: introduce yourself, get to know people, continue existing socials like Sarabi, check a marketing calendar for celebration ideas, and be the approachable go-to person.

This is maintenance, not leadership. No mention of diagnosing what NALA's culture actually needs, measuring culture health, connecting culture initiatives to business outcomes, or building systems that sustain culture without a single person driving it.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> "It's more like building on it rather than trying to create a new culture. So I'd really have a look at what NALA is all about... I'd really have a look at the whole year and see what's happening with the company."

### 6. Self-awareness on weakness

Anna identified her biggest weakness as lack of confidence to push ideas forward -- mentioned unprompted and then repeated when asked directly. This is a genuine and honest signal, but it is the exact opposite of what NALA needs in this role: someone who drives culture without waiting for permission.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> "I think just having the confidence to just go ahead with my idea and stop second-guessing myself... The worst thing she can do is say no, and then we move on."
