## Key Discussion Points

### Background and role fit

Michael has a Bachelor of Commerce in Accounting, is pursuing CPA (intermediate level), and currently works as an operational assistant at an asset management firm doing client onboarding and transactional activities. Previous consultancy at the National Treasury on cash flow mapping and P&L reconciliation. His experience is adjacent but operationally narrow - manual document checks, no automated tooling, no fintech-specific compliance experience.

> [!quote]- Source
> "I currently work at the asset management as operational assistant whereby I do mostly the onboarding of clients and also the transactional activities between the client and the company. Apart from that, I've also served as a consultant here at the National Treasury, whereby I used to do the cash flow mapping, the profit and loss reconciliation."

### Tooling and systems experience

Michael's tooling experience is limited to internal proprietary systems (NAGAS), Excel/spreadsheets, and Microsoft Teams/Outlook for communication. No experience with Jira, case management platforms, or automated KYC/KYB screening tools. His current KYC process is entirely manual with a paper checklist methodology. He identified the gap himself but has not solved it.

> [!quote]- Source
> "When it comes to KYC, our things are still being done manually. So you have to go through documents manually. We have a KYC methodology which provides all the requirements that we are supposed to request from the client."

> [!quote]- Source (on tooling)
> "I've used the Novasheet. I've also been able to use the Excel sheet on updating the client's details. And I think the only thing I can say that we do lack, but it has been in my mind, and that I think it needs to be introduced - maybe something that a system that can be able to track on the missing documents and give you a reminder."

### Inherited queue prioritisation (scenario question)

When given the scenario of inheriting a queue with document reviews, unresolved screening, sales pressure, and compliance demands, Michael acknowledged the pressure but responded with generic prioritisation language. He mentioned prioritising urgent items, creating a checklist, and communicating with salespeople about compliance requirements. No structured framework, no mention of risk-based triage, no discussion of how to assess which cases need immediate attention vs. which can wait.

> [!quote]- Source
> "I do prioritize those that are doing any day first, and I do create a checklist in order that I can be able to mark or check those that have been able to backfill."

> [!quote]- Source (on where the checklist lives)
> "You can use an Excel sheet. And also I can allocate the task in terms of days and locate them in my calendar so that I can be able to easily track them and have a target on how I'm supposed to do them and the deliverables."

### Handling pressure to approve a non-compliant vendor

Asked twice how he would handle sales pushing for fast approval on a non-compliant vendor. First answer was procedural (explain requirements, frame compliance as beneficial). When Sidi pushed for how he would handle the interpersonal pressure specifically, Michael's answer was to seek agreement with the manager rather than demonstrating conviction or independent judgment.

> [!quote]- Source (first attempt)
> "I will explain to the salesperson the required ways of - or the required recommendations so that you can be able to follow through and get the required documents and the procedures that are supposed to go through."

> [!quote]- Source (when pushed)
> "I think the best way is to try and come to a point where you can agree on something. So you have to prepare a discussion with the manager and explain to him or her the needs that are - the requirements that are being there and the missing documents that are supposed to be provided so that you can be able to agree on the same and come up with a clear solution."

### Strongest moment: compliance escalation from the assessment

Michael's most engaged answer was about the technical assessment question on a colleague who used an expired certificate of incorporation and missed sanctions screening. He described following a reporting and escalation process. This was the one area where he showed some genuine interest and competence, though the answer remained procedural rather than analytical.

> [!quote]- Source
> "The point where we have found a colleague that has not served the sanctions, and the colleague has used an expired certificate of incorporation to board in our merchant. So you have to follow through the process, the reporting process, from when it has happened, what you're supposed to do immediately, and the escalation - I mean, the reporting level that you're supposed to go through and how we recommend that."
