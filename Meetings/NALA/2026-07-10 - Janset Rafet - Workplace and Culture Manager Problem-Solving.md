## Key Discussion Points

### Janset's background and current role at Revolut

Janset manages the London office (115,000 sq ft, 440 desks, 58 meeting rooms, 4 floors) and Dublin remotely (44 desks in a WeWork). She manages 2 team members and 12 vendors. Her manager left early in her tenure and she absorbed both office management and facilities management responsibilities.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> **Janset Rafet:** "So I have the London office, which is 115,000 square feet. It's 440 desks, 58 meeting rooms. It's an amazing building. We have 4 floors."
> 
> **Janset Rafet:** "So when I joined as an office manager, my manager left quite early, like a few weeks in and she left. I was put deep in the water, dive deep, figure out yourself, and I started taking on what my manager was doing."

### Motivation for leaving Revolut

Career progression is the primary driver. Janset wants to create processes from scratch in a startup rather than navigate Revolut's established systems. She framed it as legacy-building.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> **Janset Rafet:** "This will give me an opportunity with my knowledge, with my experience, to go and create something of my own that I'll be proud of. And then like, even I told Owen, like, even in 10 years I might not be there anymore, but my process, something that I've created, will be there."
> 
> **Janset Rafet:** "My team thinks I'm ready for progression, but there isn't a job for me to progress to."

### Problem scenario 1: Handling processes that become obsolete quickly

Susanna described NALA's challenge of policies going stale before they get reviewed. Janset's response was attitudinal -- accept, iterate, learn -- rather than structured.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> **Janset Rafet:** "For me, it's all about learning. And then if you create a small process, if it gets looked after 3 months, if it's not relevant... at least you have a base. So quickly go back to it."
> 
> **Janset Rafet:** "If you just calmly accept everything... it's all about learning, and it's a benefit for me to actually go back, redo it with the feedback given from other teams."

### Problem scenario 2: Event approval bottleneck with C-suite

When asked how she'd handle C-suite delayed approvals with events approaching, Janset proposed preparing a complete business case upfront to minimise back-and-forth.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> **Janset Rafet:** "I want to have absolutely everything looked at from every corner, have already go with a finalized event, and then I just -- my simple ask at the end will be yes, no, can we proceed, any feedback. But I will go with something that's ready, and even if they take months to look at it, even if it's like a week before the event, at least I'll have everything ready."

### Problem scenario 3: Managing Benji's high standards and perceived micromanagement

Susanna gave significant context about Benji's demanding style (aesthetics, content production, fast turnaround). Janset's answer was generic adaptability.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> **Janset Rafet:** "I just adapt to people's personalities so easily because like I'm an open book. There's nothing I hide, everything's out here, you see it. The way you want to work, it's fine by me because I can just adapt to your preferences, your schedule, to absolutely everything."

### Tenure pattern and retention risk

Susanna directly probed the ~1 year tenure at each employer. Janset explained the progression logic (promoted at Foxtons after 1 year, promoted at Workspace after 1 year, hit ceiling at Revolut after 1 year). Susanna also flagged that the NALA role may not offer obvious progression, and Janset's answer relied on startup growth creating future opportunities.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> **Susanna Bae:** "I looked at the length of your employment and it's like about a year or so in each... I wondered whether like it was intentional that you change your jobs like after a year or so."
> 
> **Janset Rafet:** "For me, why I was interested in a startup company was because it's sort of like it can't really get boring because the company keeps growing... you never know, one day the team can get bigger."

### Punctuality and organisation

Janset's strongest self-assessment. She also described managing her junior report's organisation on top of her own.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> **Janset Rafet:** "I've never been late to work, not even once. And I always go early because I like a coffee and a cigarette before I start work."
> 
> **Janset Rafet:** "I have to plan my day and I have to plan his day as well. So, my responsibilities, I need to remember what he needs to do as well."

### What attracted Janset to NALA

People-centric employer brand. She noticed NALA's social media focuses on employees and culture rather than just metrics.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> **Janset Rafet:** "You go to NALA and you see all the pictures of the employees, you see all the little gatherings... people who work there who are happy, which is really nice because you don't get to see it every time."
