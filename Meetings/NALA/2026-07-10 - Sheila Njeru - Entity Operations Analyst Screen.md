# 2026-07-10 - Sheila Njeru - Entity Operations Analyst - Talent Screen

> [!summary] TL;DR
> Talent screen for the Entity Operations Analyst role (1-year contract, Nairobi office). Sheila has broad compliance and licensing experience across ~10 African jurisdictions (Equity Bank, Upside, Rafiki Group), covering license renewals, regulatory reporting, SARs/STRs, and CBK submissions. ## Assessment

### Compliance & licensing breadth
Sheila claims experience across ~10 jurisdictions (DRC, Uganda, South Africa, Burundi, Tanzania, South Sudan, Kenya, and others) and multiple regulatory bodies. Her background spans Equity Bank (compliance analyst, license renewals, PCI DSS), Upside (regulatory reporting, SARs/STRs to FRC), and involvement with Rafiki Group on PSB licensing in Kenya.

**Signal:** The breadth is real but depth is hard to verify. Descriptions were high-level and frequently interrupted by audio issues. She listed activities (reporting, renewals, document requests) rather than demonstrating mastery of systems or frameworks.

> [!quote]- Source
> "We were making sure that the license for Equity Bank is renewed and also we don't lose the license from the comments which you are getting."

### Scenario response: licence tracker cleanup
When given a scenario (join NALA, find outdated tracker, no document owners, scattered files, fix in 7 days), Sheila described a reasonable first step -- gap analysis and chasing document owners -- but could not close the loop. When Sidi pressed on how she would close within 7 days, she stalled. She did not mention centralising documents, building a RACI, or creating a single source of truth.

> [!quote]- Source
> "Person is to come up with a checklist with the already available systems whereby we'll be able to lower the gaps. Let's say the documents are not printed, then I start getting into who is responsible for this missing document."

### Document management and version control
When asked how she would make a licence filing folder audit-ready, retrievable, and version-controlled, Sheila's answers were thin. For audit-readiness she mentioned folder naming and access restrictions. For version control, she confused the concept with software licence expiry -- describing a scenario where a tool's licence lapses and documents become inaccessible. This is a gap.

> [!quote]- Source (version control confusion)
> "I make sure that it's expired, that is the license, license of the software we are using. We request the approval early so that we don't get that we have several documents in like a certain software, then when we need to retrieve them, the license has expired."

### Prioritisation under pressure
Asked for a concrete example of juggling urgent tasks, Sheila initially gave a generic framework (prioritise by directorate/jurisdiction, then by licence vs government requests vs business development). Sidi pushed for a real scenario. She described a situation where licence renewal, a DCI information request, and new business onboarding competed -- and she prioritised licence first, then government agency, then business. The logic is sound but the storytelling was weak and needed significant prompting.

> [!quote]- Source
> "I have to prioritize the license ecosystem, meaning without it you cannot do the other two."

### Escalation and proactive communication
When asked how she handles unresponsive senior stakeholders, Sheila's answer was brief: "It depends on the deadline... if the deadline is approaching, I have to escalate." Sidi asked for a scenario and she mentioned escalating to a personal assistant. The answer lacked nuance -- no mention of finding alternative routes, building relationships, or pre-empting blockers. When asked what would make her raise her hand early, she described regulatory comments going unimplemented year-over-year, which shows some awareness of systemic risk but was not a strong answer.

> [!quote]- Source
> "It depends on the deadline, the deadline of the request I made. If the deadline is approaching, I have to escalate."

### Tools and systems
She cited Smartsheet (Microsoft ecosystem) and "MI" (described as an advanced indexation/alert system, likely a custom compliance tracker or Microsoft-based tool). She described automated alerts for deadlines and traffic-light status dashboards. Reasonable for the level but not distinctive.

> [!quote]- Source
> "It's a Smartsheet, it's powered by Microsoft. It's an end-to-end. Mainly we use MI because MI is able to bring an alert when somebody has done, has inputted that data before the deadline."
