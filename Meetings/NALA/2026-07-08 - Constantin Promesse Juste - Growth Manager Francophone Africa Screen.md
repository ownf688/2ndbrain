## Key discussion points

### Background and diaspora connections

Constantin is Rwandan-origin, raised in Belgium, now in Paris. He founded a nonprofit doing Web3/tech education for African children and has connections across Rwanda, Kenya, Uganda, and the broader Pan-African community in France and Belgium. He has personal experience with remittance pain points ("my family, me myself also send a lot of money home and we get ripped by Western Union").

> [!quote]- Source
> "I went to all the countries where you went. So, for example, I went to Uganda, Kenya, Rwanda, all those countries. My family lives in those countries."

### Growth scenario - event recruitment

When asked how he'd recruit 100 Rwandans in Paris for a NALA event using only phone, taxi, and WhatsApp (no social media), Constantin identified: (1) church congregations of his pastor uncles (200-500 people), (2) Pan-African groups he's already part of, (3) friends' networks, and (4) African import shops. Solid first-pass but lacked specificity on sequencing or conversion tactics.

> [!quote]- Source
> "The most easy one is directly to go to the congregation of all my uncles who pastor and so on, because it's big congregations. So they have maybe 200, 350, there are 500 sometimes."

### Growth scenario - conversion analysis

When presented with the scenario of 45 sign-ups but zero first transfers, Constantin's initial response focused on demonstrating the product at the event (which was a past-tense action, not a diagnosis). With prompting from Ryan, he landed on trust and habits as barriers, and suggested maintaining ongoing community channels post-event. He didn't independently identify other common blockers like KYC friction, corridor availability, or recipient-side issues.

> [!quote]- Source
> "I would say trust or habits."

### Growth mistake reflection

Constantin shared a story about presenting blockchain concepts too technically to a non-technical audience. Good self-awareness about audience calibration, though the example is fairly basic.
