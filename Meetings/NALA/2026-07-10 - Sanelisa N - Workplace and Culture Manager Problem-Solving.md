## Key Discussion Points

### Scenario 1: Unresponsive C-suite blocking a customer event (next week deadline)

Sanelisa's approach was to parallel-track: chase approvals while simultaneously starting vendor outreach and planning. She asked smart qualifying questions (budget status, customer comms, repeat event, existing supplier relationships, company card availability) before proposing action.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> **Sanelisa:** "I'll chase again to see if I can not get any approvals. If they're not responding, I'll reach out to you and see, is there any delays, any way that you could escalate it just to kind of go at it from different angles. And while I wait for that response, I'll go ahead and start planning. I'll start reaching out to suppliers and I'll plan as if I've had approval."
>
> **Sanelisa:** "Would not notify the customers yet though, because I don't want it to be, you know, planning this, they know, and then they don't, you know, it doesn't show up."
>
> **Sanelisa:** "Depending on my understanding of the C-suite, you know, what their personalities are like, I might say, hey, I'm just gonna go ahead and I've started the organization, please let me know before I put payments down. Just honesty and transparency is important."

### Scenario 2: Broken AC during the hottest two weeks of the year (WeWork office)

Immediately identified the health and safety dimension. Structured her response around: assess the weather risk, escalate to WeWork for alternative space, communicate options to the team, involve Owen/People team for the final call.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> **Sanelisa:** "Number one, look at how hot it's going to be. I remember a couple weeks ago we had red alert, amber alerts, and that for me presents a health and safety risk. So I would speak to whoever has the ultimate responsibility when it comes to health and safety of this team."
>
> **Sanelisa:** "WeWork is getting a very strong email. Asking what's gone wrong, and most importantly, what can they offer us in the moment? We have an immediate need for a cooler space."
>
> **Sanelisa:** "If it's not safe for the staff, that is a big problem. That is potentially a compliance issue. See if they have a nearby office that we could use, or an alternative that is in the same building."

Unbundled a single-package facilities/cleaning/maintenance contract. Gave the incumbent vendor feedback and chances to improve. When standards remained inconsistent, reviewed the contract terms, sourced compliant replacements (ESG/CSR aligned), separated cleaning as a direct contract, brought maintenance under the building's existing engineer, and achieved both cost savings and quality improvement.

> [!quote]- Verbatim
> **Sanelisa:** "I'm happy. I think facilities, depending on the lease you have, facilities are something that you really should invest in. So it's not about paying this amount of money, but if we're paying this amount of money, I expect a certain standard of space."
>
> **Sanelisa:** "I gave them the opportunity to improve, gave my feedback, tried to work on them with it. And there'd be times when they'd address something like, for example, the coffee machine not being cleaned. They'd address it, but then the following week it's back to what it was before."
>
> **Sanelisa:** "The building engineer is already in our office doing more than he should for us, understand the building well and so much cheaper. So I went with them and yeah, ended up saving 160-something."
