# Decoupling performance scoring from compensation prevents managers optimizing for pay over honest assessment

In NALA's previous performance cycle, some execs wanted to change scores after seeing the compensation implications — optimizing the output (pay) rather than the input (honest assessment). Owen's Earn Your Spot framework deliberately separates the two: managers score purely on performance (skills + impact) and values, then Peter sets a reward budget, and the two are mapped together mechanically.

The design principle: if managers know a "3" means a 5% raise and a "4" means 10%, they'll game the score to get the pay outcome they want for their person. If they don't know the financial mapping until after calibration, the score reflects actual performance.

**Implication:** Any performance system where the scorer can see the financial outcome at scoring time is incentive-incompatible. Separate the scoring from the reward allocation, and make the mapping a leadership decision, not a manager decision.
