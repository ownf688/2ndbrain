## Themes — What's Working

- Technical grooming sessions improving alignment across engineering teams
- Good delivery pace; dedicated refactoring time appreciated
- Strong team spirit and responsive colleagues
- Mission remains exciting and motivating
- 1:1 feedback is fair and contributes to growth
- Mobile chapter making progress on technical excellence (linting, CI/CD)

## Themes — What's NOT Working (critical signals)

**Strategic direction and prioritisation (3/6 responses):**
- "We do not seem to be capable of prioritising... trying to do too much, to not a high enough standard"
- "Plans are changing very quickly and very often"
- "Feels like there are many bets but we can't stick with one long enough to make it work"
- "The vision and mission changes often, and the most important priorities change quarter to quarter"
- "Uncertainty with the steps on how we get there... This same feeling/vibe is coming from other people as well"

**Talent attrition (1/6 but sharp):**
- "We are losing great people all of the time, due to frustration and lack of direction"
- "There are still some amazing people that work here, despite lots of high quality people leaving"

**Culture signalling (1/6):**
- "We are very loud about things. Companies that have good morale don't need to tell employees that things are good, or that the company is doing a good job at something. That's felt, not told. In actual fact, being told those things ends up demoralising people."

**Documentation lag:**
- "Keeping documentation and processes up to date as our systems evolve"

## So What

Half the respondents flag the same issue: **NALA changes direction too often and can't prioritise**. This isn't a single disgruntled voice — it's a pattern. Combined with the attrition signal, this is the kind of data that belongs in the Cost of Mediocrity tracker and in Owen's next exec conversation with [[Benji]] or [[Markus Seebacher]].

The "loud about things" comment is a culture signal worth flagging to [[Peter Gulliver]] — if internal comms are landing as performative rather than genuine, that's a brand problem inside the company.
