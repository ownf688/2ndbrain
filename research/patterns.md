# Phase 2: Patterns

Built 2026-10-03 from the 17 files in `research/scan/`. A pattern counts only if it shows up in **at least 2 scan areas** and rests on **at least 3 independent sources**. Numbers are taken from the scan files, which link the originals. **Most figures were seen in search results quoting the original page, not checked on the page itself** (see `assumptions.md` #9).

Strength key: **Strong** = many sources, official data, clear direction. **Medium** = real, but mixed or mostly company/news data. **Weak** = qualifies on the rule but thin.

## P1. Dated legal deadlines are landing on small firms, and most have real penalties (Strong)

**Areas:** laws, work/hiring, housing, consumer/retail, food/drink, population, travel, Africa.
**Sources (independent):** Employment Rights Act timetable (Hill Dickinson, Blake Morgan, Wilsons); Companies House ID verification (Law Debenture; Companies House management info via search: only 55% of directors verified by end June 2026); MTD for Income Tax (LITRG, UHY); Renters' Rights Act (Cheffins, Crawley BC); DMCC subscription rules (Harper James, Subscription Insider); Martyn's Law (Gowling, Irwin Mitchell); packaging EPR fees (gov.uk); Data (Use and Access) Act complaints duty (CMS); Nigeria VAT on foreign digital sellers (vatcalc).

**The key dates (next 15 months):**
| Date | Rule | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Oct 2026 (live) | Tribunal claim window 3 to 6 months | GB employers |
| 30 Oct 2026 | Duty to take reasonable steps to prevent sexual harassment | GB employers |
| 17 Nov 2026 | Companies House ID verification transition ends; about 6 to 7m people in scope | Every UK company director/PSC, NI included |
| 15 Dec 2026 | Landlord database opens (England) | Private landlords |
| 1 Jan 2027 | Unfair dismissal qualifying period 2 years to 6 months; compensation cap removed | GB employers |
| Jan 2027 | Subscription contract rules (DMCC Act) | Any UK firm selling subscriptions |
| Spring 2027 | Martyn's Law (venues 200+ capacity) | UK-wide incl. NI |
| 6 Apr 2027 | MTD Income Tax at £30k; unused pensions count for inheritance tax | Sole traders, landlords, estates |

**Cause and effect:** Government wants more revenue and fewer abuses (fraud, bad landlords, unfair dismissal) without new spending. It pushes the work onto firms through rules with dates. Small firms have no compliance staff. So they either ignore it, pay a professional, or buy something that does the job for them.

**Knock-on, one step out:** a rush of demand just before each date, then a cliff. Professionals (accountants, HR consultants, letting agents) get swamped and look for ways to process clients in bulk.
**Knock-on, two steps out:** the firms that got caught out create a second market (tribunal claims, fines, blocked filings), and insurers start pricing in the new risks (for example employment-practices cover once dismissal claims become uncapped).

**Counter-evidence:** deadlines slip (EU AI Act high-risk moved to Dec 2027; EUDR delayed twice; Ireland missed pay transparency). The EU cut sustainability reporting scope by about 90%. Big incumbents (Xero, Sage, BrightHR, Citation, payroll vendors) ship updates for free inside existing products. **ERA 2025 and Renters' Rights do not apply in Northern Ireland.**

## P2. Labour at the bottom of the market got dearer and scarcer at the same time (Strong)

**Areas:** work/hiring, food/drink (hospitality), population (migration, care), health (care staffing), consumer.
**Sources:** ONS labour market (payrolled employees -145k y/y to 30.2m, Aug 2026); Bank of England Decision Maker Panel (after the NICs rise 29% cut jobs, 64% cut margins); National Living Wage £12.71 (Apr 2026); Home Office visa stats (Skilled Worker main applicants -39%); ONS migration (net 171k, down from 331k); UKHospitality (170k jobs lost, lobby figure); BBPA (pub closures +26%); LaingBuisson/Which? (care home fees £1,371 to £1,607 a week).

**Cause and effect:** Employer National Insurance rose (Apr 2025), the minimum wage kept rising faster than prices, and overseas recruitment was cut back hard (July 2025 visa rules). So each low-paid worker costs more and is harder to find. Firms respond by cutting margins first, then hiring less, then cutting roles.

**One step out:** firms look for anything that replaces a staff hour: self-serve, automation, outsourced "done for you" work. Care and hospitality prices rise (care fees, pub closures).
**Two steps out:** young people find fewer entry roles (see P4), and the care squeeze hits older people's wealth (see P6).

**Counter-evidence:** most firms absorbed the NICs rise in margins rather than job cuts (BoE: 53% said they would cut jobs, 29% did). Unemployment at 4.9% is still low historically. Migration figures are provisional and often revised up.

## P3. AI made making things cheap, so the value moves to finished outcomes, trust and distribution (Strong)

**Areas:** AI/software, media/creators, solo founders, laws (AI labelling), fashion (human-made labels).
**Sources:** Epoch AI (price for a fixed level of model performance falls about 40x a year); reported revenue of Cursor ($4bn+), Lovable (~$600m), Replit (~$525m); software fund IGV -28% then recovered ("SaaSpocalypse"); Intercom Fin $0.99 per resolved conversation; Deezer (AI tracks 50%+ of uploads but 1 to 3% of listening); YouTube demonetised mass-produced content (Jul 2025); Pew (clicks 8% with an AI summary vs 15% without); Similarweb (no-click news searches 56% to 69%); Carta (solo founders 36.3% of new startups, H1 2025); TrustMRR (verified solo revenue).

**Cause and effect:** Building software and producing content now costs a fraction of what it did. So supply floods in (apps, AI music, AI articles, AI job applications). When supply is unlimited, buyers can't tell good from bad, and what becomes scarce is: (a) someone who takes responsibility for the result, (b) a trusted name, and (c) a way to reach buyers.

**One step out:** pricing shifts from "per seat" to "per result". Search traffic to websites collapses ("Google zero"), so owned audiences (email lists, newsletters, communities) gain value. beehiiv paid subscriptions +84%.
**Two steps out:** platforms and regulators add labelling and authenticity rules (EU AI Act Article 50, live Aug 2026). People pay for verification and provenance. Copying becomes trivial, so any tool without distribution or data gets cloned in weeks.

**Counter-evidence:** the biggest money is still in *tools* (Anthropic $65bn+ run-rate, Cursor, Chatbase $864k MRR). Software stocks recovered by June 2026. No evidence anyone pays a premium for a "human-made" label (8+ rival labels, no price data). Agent workloads use more tokens, so AI bills can rise.

## P4. The entry-level job market is squeezed and the hiring funnel is flooded (Strong)

**Areas:** work/hiring, AI/software, population (fewer migrants), solo founders (AI headshots for job seekers).
**Sources:** ONS (youth unemployment 16.2%, 11-year high); Indeed Hiring Lab (graduate postings about -7% y/y, lowest since 2020); Institute of Student Employers (140 applications per graduate vacancy, +14%; vacancies -7%); Gartner via secondaries (39% of job seekers used AI to apply); Stanford "Canaries" (about 13% relative fall for 22 to 25s in AI-exposed jobs); DfE (apprenticeship starts +8.7%).

**Cause and effect:** Entry roles cost more (P2) and some junior tasks are done by AI. At the same time AI makes applying almost free, so each applicant sends many more applications. Result: fewer roles, many more applications per role, and the signal in a CV collapses.

**One step out:** employers stop trusting CVs and cover letters and move to work samples, live assessments and identity checks. Small employers, who can't afford enterprise tools, drown.
**Two steps out:** interview fraud (proxy candidates, deepfakes) becomes a real cost; regulators look at AI in hiring (EU AI Act lists hiring AI as high-risk from Dec 2027).

**Counter-evidence:** it is a squeeze, not a collapse. NEETs are flat (13.0%), 30% of big graduate employers are hiring *more*, apprenticeships are up. The measured cause is cost more than AI. Fraud numbers are mostly vendor marketing.

## P5. A gas price shock pushed inflation and rates back up, and households are buying their way out of energy bills (Strong)

**Areas:** energy, markets, housing, consumer, food (energy costs), Ireland/NI.
**Sources:** Ofgem (price cap +13% Jul, +4% Oct 2026, typical £1,723); Utility Regulator NI (Power NI +6.2%); ONS CPI (3.1% Aug 2026, fuels +6.0%); Fed (hike to 3.75 to 4.00%, Sep 2026); ECB (deposit rate 2.50%); BoE (held 3.75%, vote 6-3); BoE money and credit (new mortgage rate 4.60%, approvals down); MCS (210k installs H1 2026, +17%); SEAI (Irish solar applications +76%); Ofgem BUS report (heat pump applications +19%); Warm Homes Plan (rented homes EPC C by 2030, £10k cap).

**Cause and effect:** The Strait of Hormuz has been largely blocked since late Feb 2026. Gas prices jumped. Energy pushed UK inflation up from 2.6% to 3.1% in two months. The Fed and ECB hiked; the BoE is split. Mortgage costs edge up. Households already moving to solar and batteries keep going, and Ireland's grant-led market surges.

**One step out:** demand for anything that cuts gas use (heat pumps, batteries, insulation) and for advice on which upgrade pays back. Landlords face a separate legal push (EPC C by 2030) that does not depend on prices.
**Two steps out:** higher rates squeeze house buyers and small-firm borrowing; the consumer confidence recovery (GfK -13) could reverse.

**Counter-evidence:** the solar record run started in 2025, *before* the shock. It is mostly a **gas** shock (gas +24%, electricity +5% in July), so solar payback barely changes. A Hormuz reopening would cut prices fast. Bills are a three-year high, not a record.

## P6. Older people hold the wealth, and rules dated April 2027 will make it move (Strong)

**Areas:** population, housing, markets, health (care).
**Sources:** ONS Wealth and Assets (median wealth £502,500 for heads aged 65 to 74, 33x the youngest; older wave); Resolution Foundation (boomers hold over half of wealth; inheritances peak about 2035; gap narrowed by £86,400); HMRC via news (IHT £7.7bn Apr 2025 to Feb 2026, OBR forecast £14.7bn by 2031); RSM/MoneyWeek (pension IHT from 6 Apr 2027, 10,000+ extra estates); Key (over-65s £2.94tn property, vendor source); LPS NI HPI (+9.2% y/y); Budget 2025 (cash ISA cut to £12k for under-65s only).

**Cause and effect:** Decades of house price rises and final-salary pensions put the money with people now in their 60s and 70s. Government wants a slice: from April 2027 unused pensions count for inheritance tax. Care fees (£1,371+/week) and the tax both eat into estates, so families have a reason to plan, gift or spend *now*.

**One step out:** a surge in demand for financial advice, gifting records, will-writing, probate and care-fee planning, mostly from adult children aged 45 to 60 acting for parents.
**Two steps out:** a long inheritance wave peaking around 2035 lifts probate and estate-admin work; regional effects where house prices are rising fast (NI +9.2%).

**Counter-evidence:** most of this wealth is locked in houses. The age gap in wealth is narrowing slightly. The average heir is 61, so the "young people get rich" story is wrong. Regulated financial advice needs FCA authorisation.

## P7. Second-hand and social shopping are growing fast, but the profit is thin (Medium)

**Areas:** consumer/retail, fashion.
**Sources:** Vinted accounts via FashionUnited (revenue €1.1bn +38%, profit -19%); ThredUp 2026 report (US resale +19% in 2025, forecast ~7%/yr); ECDB (TikTok Shop UK ~€2.6bn, estimate); Whatnot (UK first-time buyers +374%, company claim); EU destruction ban (Jul 2026, large firms).

**Cause and effect:** Squeezed budgets plus cheap-to-list apps plus a culture that rewards thrift. The platforms are buying growth with marketing. Duty changes make cheap imports slightly dearer in the EU (UK not until Oct 2028).

**One step out:** millions of part-time sellers need help listing, pricing and shipping; small brands want their own buy-back/resale.
**Two steps out:** platforms add the same help as free features (Vinted, eBay already have listing aids), squeezing tool makers.

**Counter-evidence:** growth is forecast to slow sharply; Shein still grows; UK keeps the £135 duty relief till 2028; most social-commerce data is company claims.

## P8. Scarce, real-life and named-human experiences gain value, but only at the top (Medium)

**Areas:** travel/leisure, fashion/culture, media, food/drink.
**Sources:** LIVE trade body (UK live music £7.6bn, +12.9%); Music Venue Trust (over half of small venues unprofitable, 2.5% margin); AIF (249 festivals lost since 2019); Live Nation 10-K ($25.2bn, +9%); LTA (padel courts +78%, players to 860k); Strava (running clubs 3.5x); BPI (vinyl £174.4m, +19.9%); Barclays (entertainment spend +4.3%, essentials -2.3%); beehiiv/Substack (paid subscriptions growing); IWSR (alcohol -2%, alcohol-free +9%).

**Cause and effect:** When content and goods are endless and cheap, people spend on what can't be copied: being there, playing with others, a named person they trust. But the money concentrates in the biggest names and newest formats.

**One step out:** booking, membership and community tooling for fast-growing grassroots formats (padel, run clubs, small events); face-value resale once touting is banned.
**Two steps out:** oversupply risk (padel courts built faster than players arrive); grassroots venues keep closing.

**Counter-evidence:** experience spend +4.3% is barely above inflation; grassroots ticket prices flat at £11.56; no evidence of a "human-made" price premium.

## P9. Defence money is arriving in Northern Ireland fast, but almost none reaches small firms yet (Medium)

**Areas:** geopolitics/defence, Ireland/NI.
**Sources:** MoD regional expenditure 2024/25 and 2025/26 (NI £271m then £464m, +63% real, largest per-person rise; SMEs £2m in 2024/25); Thales/gov.uk (£1.16bn LMM contract, up to ~£1.6bn, 200 jobs); Defence Industrial Strategy (SME spend to £7.5bn by May 2028); NATO Hague declaration (5% by 2035); NICEI (NI output record, +3.6% y/y); NISRA ASHE (NI pay +7.4%).

**Cause and effect:** War in Ukraine and NATO targets lift UK defence budgets. Thales Belfast makes the missiles Ukraine needs, so NI gets a big, lumpy increase. Government says it wants SMEs to get more, but in NI the money flows to one prime.

**One step out:** Thales and other primes need more local suppliers; SMEs need to pass security and cyber checks (Cyber Essentials Plus) and find tenders.
**Two steps out:** NI wage pressure (already +7.4%) and skills shortages in engineering.

**Counter-evidence:** NI is still under half the UK per-head average; growth hangs on one Ukraine-linked contract; a ceasefire could slow orders. Defence buying is slow, vetted and relationship-led, which is bad for a solo outsider.

## P10. Borders and trade are getting stickier (Medium)

**Areas:** consumer, fashion, geopolitics, travel, Africa, Ireland/NI.
**Sources:** US ended $800 de minimis (29 Aug 2025); EU €3 per item duty from 1 Jul 2026; UK £135 relief ends Oct 2028; US 10% UK vs 15% EU tariff; UK ETA £10 to £16 to £20; EU Entry/Exit System live Apr 2026, ETIAS due Q4 2026; Nigeria 7.5% VAT on foreign digital sellers (Jan 2026); Windsor Framework green lane (May 2025).

**Cause and effect:** Governments are taxing and checking cross-border flows more: cheap parcels, travellers, digital services. Each rule adds a form, a fee or a check.

**One step out:** small sellers and travellers need help with landed cost, duty and permits.
**Two steps out:** NI's dual-market position may become an advantage (UK tariff rate to the US), but no data shows trade diversion yet.

**Counter-evidence:** the amounts are small (€3 per item; £20 permit); big platforms and carriers absorb the complexity for their customers.

## P11. Weight-loss drugs are changing *what* people buy more than *how much* (Medium)

**Areas:** health, food/drink, consumer.
**Sources:** Worldpanel by Numerator (6.3% of UK households have a user; about £780m a year less grocery spend); Food Foundation (about 7% of UK adults have used them); KFF (12% of US adults currently taking one); Cornell/Numerator (US grocery spend -5.5% in 6 months); Circana (effect mostly gone after a year); Reuters (food firms mentioning GLP-1s on earnings calls up about 2.5x); Lilly price rise (top UK dose £122 to £330/month).

**Cause and effect:** Cheaper, easier drugs (pills at ~$149/month in the US; generic semaglutide in India/China/Canada from 2026) spread use. Users eat less snack food and more protein. Food firms reformulate and shrink packs.

**One step out:** protein and portion-control products; support for people coming off the drugs.
**Two steps out:** clothing sizes, gym and insurance changes (not found in this scan).

**Counter-evidence:** £780m is under 1% of UK grocery spend; Greggs still grows; much of the cut reverses within a year. UK online prescribing is regulated (GPhC rules, Feb 2025).

## P12. Central banks buy gold and emerging-market users hold digital dollars, but rich-country retail is not following (Weak)

**Areas:** markets, Africa/emerging markets.
**Sources:** World Gold Council (central banks 289t in Q2 2026, +62%; retail bars/coins -3%); gold price peak $5,501.70 then about -24%; stablecoin supply about $305bn, down about $10bn since May 2026; Chainalysis (stablecoins 43% of Sub-Saharan Africa crypto volume); BoE proposed £20k holding limit; cedi +40%, naira +8%.

**Cause and effect:** Sanctions risk and dollar distrust push central banks to gold. In weak-currency countries, people use dollar stablecoins to save and send money. As those currencies recover, that reason weakens.

**Counter-evidence:** strong. Retail gold demand fell; stablecoin supply shrank; UK rules cap retail holdings. **Conflict of interest:** this is the user's employer's market (remittances and payments). Excluded from openings (assumption #5).

## How the patterns connect

```
 Gulf conflict ─► P5 gas shock ─► inflation/rates up ─► squeezed households & firms
                                                              │
 Tax + wage + visa rules ─► P2 dear, scarce labour ───────────┤
                                                              ▼
 AI 40x cheaper/yr ─► P3 making is cheap ─► buyers pay for OUTCOMES, TRUST, DISTRIBUTION
        │                                         ▲
        └──► P4 flooded hiring funnel ◄── P2      │
                                                  │
 Govt wants revenue w/o spending ─► P1 dated rules on small firms ─► "do it for me" demand
        │                               ▲
        └──► P6 April 2027 pension-IHT ─┘ (old wealth must move)

 War/NATO ─► P9 NI defence money (to primes, not SMEs)
 Thrift + cheap listing ─► P7 resale        Endless content ─► P8 scarce real-life/named humans
 Tariffs/permits ─► P10 sticky borders      GLP-1 pills/generics ─► P11 diet shift
```

**The central knot:** P1 + P2 + P3 point the same way. Small firms face more dated obligations (P1) with fewer, dearer staff (P2), while AI makes it cheap for an outsider to do the job for them (P3). That is where the strongest solo openings sit.
