## Evidence pointing the other way
- Gold is about 24% below its January peak. Q2 bar and coin demand fell 3% and jewellery fell 17%. The retail "flight to gold" has cooled.
- Stablecoins *shrank* by about $10bn after May 2026, even with GENIUS in place and higher rates making reserves more profitable for issuers.
- UK core inflation is flat at 2.6%. If energy eases, the BoE may not need to hike.
- The BoE holding limits (£20k per person) cap how much retail UK money could move into sterling stablecoins.

## Hypotheses tested
- **(j) Gold and digital dollars: MIXED. It splits by who is buying.**
  - **Central banks, mostly emerging markets plus Poland: SUPPORTED, strongly.** 289 t in Q2 2026, +62% year on year (WGC).
  - **Institutions and ETFs: MIXED.** Huge inflows into the January peak, then a sell-off in Q2. ETF tonnage not found.
  - **Retail in rich countries: NOT SUPPORTED recently.** Bars and coins -3% in Q2 (though +21% in H1). UK Royal Mint data not found.
  - **Retail in emerging markets: MIXED.** Asian buying was described as "strong" ([Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-07-30/gold-demand-proves-resilient-despite-q2-selloff-central-banks-return-and)), but jewellery demand (mostly India and China) fell 17%.
  - **Digital dollars: MIXED.** There is a multi-year rise (about $305bn now) and a legal framework (GENIUS, MiCA), but supply has dipped since May 2026. Emerging-market retail is the main use case for USDT, but no per-country data was found. In the UK, retail is effectively blocked by the BoE limits.
- **(a) Energy shock: SUPPORTED at the macro level.** UK electricity, gas and fuels inflation was +6.0% in Aug 2026, and the ECB and Fed are hiking partly because of energy. Solar take-up itself was not tested.
- **(h) Older people hold the money: weakly SUPPORTED.** The ISA cut exempts over-65s, which signals that older savers are where cash is concentrated. REASONABLE GUESS.
