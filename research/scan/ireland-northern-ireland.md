## Evidence pointing the other way
- NI's defence gain is concentrated in one prime (Thales) and one product line. SMEs received £2m out of £271m, so little reaches small firms.
- NI pay is still about 7% below the UK, and NI per-head defence spend is less than a third of the UK average.
- Windsor Framework "dual access" has not yet lifted north-to-south sales in real terms.
- Ireland's tax strength rests on about 10 multinationals, and US tariffs plus possible US tax changes threaten it.

## Hypotheses tested
- **(i) Defence spending is rising fastest in NI: MIXED.** On growth it may be supported. The Thales deal (£1.16bn to £1.6bn) is the largest in its history, and one summary of MoD 2024/25 data says NI had the largest % rise of any region (not verified). On level it is not supported: £140 per person vs the £490 UK average. The rise is lumpy and goes to one firm. A conflicting UK Defence Journal headline (year unknown) says Scotland was the only region to rise.
- **(h) Older people hold the money:** not tested here.
- **(b), (c):** indirect. NI IT and business services output grew 7 to 8% a year, so local demand for software and services is rising. REASONABLE GUESS.

## Possible money angles for a solo builder (OPINION)
- Tools that help NI SMEs bid into defence supply chains: supplier registration, cyber and quality compliance (Cyber Essentials, ISO checklists). £2m of £271m going to SMEs shows the gap.
- Dual-market compliance helpers for NI firms: Windsor Framework green/red lane checks, UKCA/CE labelling, and pricing that accounts for ROI's 9% VAT vs NI's 20%.
- Lightweight apps for ROI hospitality businesses repricing after the VAT cut: menu repricing, margin tracking.
- Productised services for regional NI firms around £2m turnover that Invest NI is now targeting: grant-application and reporting software.
- All-island B2B tools that handle sterling and euro invoicing and VAT for cross-border sellers.
