## Evidence pointing the other way

- The stablecoin boom follows currency weakness. With the cedi up about 40% (2025) and the naira up about 8% (2026), the "protect my savings" reason for holding stablecoins is weaker now than at the 2024 peak (REASONABLE GUESS).
- Crypto activity fell globally in 2026 (Chainalysis "drawdown"). Emerging-market stablecoin use is a payments story, not a sign of a broad crypto boom.
- Remittance flows on the biggest US corridor fell in 2025, so "emerging markets are growing" does not mean diaspora money is growing.
- African venture funding depends on a handful of mega-deals and debt. Fewer startups are getting funded.

## Hypotheses tested

- **(j) Money moving into gold and digital dollars (stablecoins): MIXED, leaning supported for stablecoins in Africa and LatAm.**
  - Supported: stablecoins were 43% of Sub-Saharan Africa crypto volume (2025). Cross-border stablecoin value grew 77.5% to US$220bn (2026). 81% of Mexico's crypto activity is stablecoins.
  - Against: the main driver (currency collapse) has reversed in Ghana and Nigeria. Nigeria now taxes crypto profits at up to 25%. Global crypto activity fell. I did not check the 2026 Sub-Saharan Africa figure on the original page, and it may simply repeat the 2025 number.
  - Gold: no direct evidence on retail gold buying in my area. Gold mattered indirectly, because gold exports drove the cedi rally (not found beyond that).
- **(b) Software getting cheap, money moving to finished services:** loosely consistent. Value is gathering on top of free payment rails (UPI, mobile money), not in the rails themselves. REASONABLE GUESS.
- **(h) Older people hold the money:** not tested (not found).

1. A compliance tool for foreign SaaS sellers that handles Nigeria's 7.5% VAT registration and charging. The rule is new (Jan 2026) and is painful for small sellers.
2. A guide and calculator for US senders on the 1% remittance tax: cash versus digital, what is exempt. **[COI]** This overlaps with the user's employer's market. Check before building.
3. Stablecoin invoicing and payout dashboards for African and LatAm freelancers paid in USDC or USDT, for bookkeeping and tax under the new Nigerian crypto tax. **[COI]**
4. Tools for merchants built on top of UPI or mobile money (reconciliation, matching payments to invoices, small-business bookkeeping). The rails are free and huge, but the software layer above them is thin.
5. Currency-move alerts for diaspora families. Example: "the cedi is up 40%, your GBP now buys less, here's how to adjust." **[COI]**
