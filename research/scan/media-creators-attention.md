## Evidence pointing the other way

- The 1-3% stream share for AI music shows **AI supply is high but demand is low**. This points the same way as hypothesis (g), but it could also simply be platform filtering.
- Total time spent online is still rising (+10 min a day), so attention as a whole is not shrinking. It is moving to AI tools, YouTube and email.
- Licensing deals show AI firms will pay for content, but only to large archives. Small creators get nothing.
- Australia's ban may shrink youth audiences on social platforms, but enforcement leaks.

## Hypotheses tested

- **(g) "Made by humans" and real-life events are gaining value: MIXED, leaning supported.**
  - For:
    - YouTube stopped paying for templated content (Jul 2025).
    - AI music is now 50%+ of Deezer uploads but only 1-3% of listening.
    - Personality-led formats are growing fast: video podcasts on TV +75%, paid newsletters +84%, LinkedIn creator payouts tripled.
    - Money follows named humans.
  - Against:
    - No direct price data was found showing anyone pays more for a "human-made" label.
    - Platforms still allow AI content. They only demonetise low-effort volume.
    - Live-events data could not be checked (search budget ran out). Not found.
  - Plainly: the supporting evidence is **about scarcity of trust and of people with a name, not about a premium for a label**. OPINION.
- **(b) Software is getting cheap so money moves to finished services: weakly supported here.** AI answers wipe out generic content but not trusted, curated expertise. REASONABLE GUESS.

## Possible money angles for a solo builder (all OPINION)

1. A "Google zero" traffic-recovery tool for small newsletter or B2B publishers: track the share of visits from search vs AI vs email, and prompt them to capture readers into owned lists.
2. Paid-community and paid-newsletter add-ons for niche B2B writers (for example HR leaders) on beehiiv or Substack: benchmarking data products, member directories.
3. A "verified human / provenance" badge and audit trail for B2B creators and recruiters' content, as AI slop grows.
4. LinkedIn video repurposing for expert operators: turn podcasts or talks into native LinkedIn clips, targeting BrandLink-style money.
5. Age-assurance-compliant youth and parent tools ahead of a possible UK 2027 under-16 ban. Speculative until gov.uk confirms.
