# Solo founders with verified income (Apr 2025 to Oct 2026)

Scan date: 2026-10-03. Area: one-person (or nearly) businesses with income checked by a third party.

> [!warning] Research limits this session
> The network proxy blocked direct fetches of carta.com, trustmrr.com, indiehackers.com, paddle.com, globes.co.il and others, and the web-search budget ran out partway through. So every number below comes from **search-result text quoting the original page**, not from a page I opened and checked myself. I found only **8 usable cases, not the 12 to 15 asked for**. The rest are marked "not found" rather than filled from memory. Re-run with fetch access to TrustMRR and Acquire.com before acting on any number.

**What "verified" means here.** TrustMRR, and Stripe-verified Indie Hackers profiles, read revenue straight from the payment provider (Stripe and similar) with read-only access. That is third-party verification. A founder posting a screenshot of their Stripe dashboard on X (formerly Twitter) is **not verified**: screenshots can be cropped, edited, or show gross rather than net revenue. Public-company filings (for example Wix's) are verified by auditors.

## 3. Hypotheses tested

**(b) Software is cheap, so the money moves to finished services: MIXED, leaning supported at the small end.**
- For: among solo consumer products, finished-output businesses (Photo AI, Interior AI, HeadshotPro) reached $35k to $150k a month. Plain developer tools (ShipFast, DataFast) sit at $9k to $21k a month, and ShipFast is falling. GojiberryAI, which delivers leads rather than software, is at $424k MRR. (REASONABLE GUESS, small sample.)
- Against: the biggest wins are still tools. Chatbase is at $864k MRR. Base44 is an app-building tool that sold for $80M, although what it sells is "a finished app without a developer". Its success is itself proof that software is getting cheap, which is the premise of (b).
- Overall: the sample is too small and too skewed by survivorship (we only see the winners) to settle the question. (OPINION.)

**(c) Small firms are stuck on AI: indirectly supported.** Chatbase and GojiberryAI make money by packaging AI as a ready-made job for SMBs. That fits the idea that SMBs pay to have AI done for them rather than learning it themselves. (REASONABLE GUESS.)

## 4. Evidence pointing the other way
- **Survivorship bias.** TrustMRR and Indie Hackers only show founders who chose to list. The typical solo SaaS earns little or nothing. I found no median MRR figure: not found.
- **Audience drives outcomes.** Levels and Lou already had huge X followings. Their results say more about distribution (reaching buyers) than about the product category.
- **AI-wrapper decay.** Photo AI fell from about $150k to about $100k a month, and ShipFast's revenue is declining. Cheap AI also makes these products easy to copy.
- **Sale prices overstate cash.** Earnouts and seller financing grew in 2025 deals.
- **Unverified.** Almost all figures here are search-snippet reads of sources I could not open.

## 5. Possible money angles for a solo builder (OPINION)
- Sell a finished outcome to SMBs ("AI support bot set up and run for you", "qualified leads every week") rather than a self-serve tool. GojiberryAI and Chatbase show the price SMBs will pay.
- List any product on TrustMRR from day one. Verified revenue is now the currency for both trust and an eventual sale (about 2.6x revenue, 10.7x profit).
- Build a profitable niche micro-SaaS aimed at a sale. At about 2.6x revenue, $100k a year in revenue would sell for about $260k. Plan for an earnout.
- Use Supabase + React to deliver productised services in UK-local niches (trades, clinics, estate agents in Northern Ireland), where audience-led US competitors don't reach.
- Build in public. Distribution was the first-customer channel in almost every case found.
