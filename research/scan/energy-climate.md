## Evidence pointing the other way
- **The growth started before the shock.** UK H1 2025 was already a record. SEAI 2025 was +16%. MCS Jan 2026 was the second-best small-scale month on record. All of this came before the Hormuz blockade (late Feb 2026).
- **This is mainly a gas shock, not an electricity shock.** In July, GB gas rose 24% but electricity only about 5%. Solar offsets electricity, so the shock improves solar payback only a little. It improves the case for heat pumps and batteries more, through cheap off-peak tariffs.
- **Bills are high but not at crisis levels.** The October cap is a three-year high, not a record. 40% of accounts are on fixed deals and protected from the rises.
- **UK growth looks steady, not like a surge.** Solar is +14% a year. A real price-panic would usually show a sharper step up. Ireland's +76% does look like one.

## Hypotheses tested
- **(a) An energy price shock is driving home solar: MIXED.**
  - The shock is real. Wholesale gas is about 190p/therm and the cap rose +13% then +4% in 2026, both blamed on the Middle East.
  - Installs are at records: MCS March and June-July 2026 were the best months since 2012, and SEAI applications are +76%.
  - But the record run began in 2025, before the shock. Electricity, which is the price solar competes with, rose only about 5%. Falling hardware cost per kW and batteries are likely a big co-driver (REASONABLE GUESS).
  - Best reading: the shock added to a trend that was already running. It did not start it. The effect looks stronger in Ireland (+76%) than in GB (+14%).
- (b) to (j): not tested in this area.

## Possible money angles for a solo builder (OPINION)
1. **Solar and battery payback calculator for NI and ROI.** It would include Power NI tariffs and SEAI grant rules, and would collect leads to sell to installers. NI has no Ofgem cap or BUS grant, so it is badly served by GB tools.
2. **Back-office SaaS for small MCS installers.** It would handle MCS/SEAI grant paperwork, the grid connection notice (DNO forms, which tell the network operator about a new install) and handover packs. The market is concentrating (the top 10 make 36.5% of BUS applications), so small installers need tools to keep up.
3. **Battery and tariff optimiser for the 2m+ homes that already have solar.** It would schedule charging against time-of-use and export tariffs. The installed base is large and keeps growing.
4. **Energy-cost pass-through tracker for small businesses.** A simple Supabase dashboard would show how gas and electricity spikes hit each customer's margins. Volatility is high and could stay high.
