## Evidence pointing the other way
- The headline growth is concentrated in a few places. Live music's record year came from a handful of mega-tours while 30 small venues closed. UK flying set a record while NI airports shrank.
- More friction at borders (ETA, EES, ETIAS) and more tourist taxes raise the cost of a trip. That could hold back the experience boom at the margin.
- Experience spending grew only about 4% in cash terms, which is barely positive after inflation.
- NI's +9% in 2025 includes the one-off Open. 2026 may fall back.

## Hypotheses tested
- **(g) "Made by humans" and real-life events are gaining value: MIXED, leaning supported for the big end.** For: live music £7.6bn (+12.9%), entertainment card spend +4.3% while essentials fell 2.3%, padel doubling, the Open's record 278,000 crowd, and the government acting on resale prices (a sign of excess demand). Against: the gains go to large events. Small venues average a 2.5% margin, more than half are unprofitable, 249 festivals have been lost since 2019, and grassroots ticket prices are flat at £11.56. People will pay for scarce, big-name events, but not for live events in general.
- **(h) Older people hold the money:** no travel-specific data found.
