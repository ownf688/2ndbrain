# O26 - Care-fee runway and funding-options report for adult children

Opening: a report for adult children of someone going into care: how long the money lasts (self-funding), when council help kicks in, whether NHS CHC is worth pursuing, plus referral income from care-fee advisers / annuity providers / CHC firms. Who pays: families, or advisers via referral fees.

Searches used: 7 of 8 (all seen 2026-10-03).

## 1. Is someone paying for this, or a worse version, today? - PASS
- FACT: CHC claims firms charge families. Slee Blackwell publishes fixed fees, e.g. £175 + VAT to attend an assessment and £800 + VAT for checklist help ([sleeblackwell.co.uk](https://www.sleeblackwell.co.uk/?p=30197)). The search summary says one major firm charges 25% + VAT of money recovered on no-win-no-fee terms. I could not tie that figure to a named firm ([hughjames.com](https://www.hughjames.com/?p=34893), [continuing-healthcare.co.uk](https://www.continuing-healthcare.co.uk/?p=532)).
- FACT: Specialist care-fee advisers (SOLLA members, wealth firms such as Evelyn) sell care-funding advice that uses cashflow modelling ([evelyn.com](https://www.evelyn.com/services/financial-planning/life-changing-events/paying-for-care-in-later-life/), [Staffordshire CC on SOLLA](https://www.staffordshire.gov.uk/adult-social-care/paying-care/independent-financial-advice-0/regulated-advice-choosing-adviser-and)). People pay for the regulated version. I found no priced standalone "runway report" product.

## 2. Is the raw material free to everyone? - FAIL
- FACT: The capital limits (England/NI £23,250 / £14,250; Scotland £36,750 / £22,750; Wales £50,000) and regional fee averages are published free by Which? ([which.co.uk](https://www.which.co.uk/money/pensions-and-retirement/financing-later-life-care/care-home-fees-ak7lP4h1owcg)).
- OPINION: The runway sum is simple: capital, minus fees plus income, until you hit the threshold. There is no data moat.

## 3. Are rivals already selling it at a similar price? - FAIL (crowded with free, good rivals)
- FACT: Which? Later Life Care has a free "cost of care and eligibility checker". Government guidance names it as an approved resource for councils to point people to ([communitycare.co.uk](https://www.communitycare.co.uk/?p=170480)).
- FACT: Autumna runs a free later-life funding advice line ([autumna.co.uk](https://autumna.co.uk/free-advice-lines/funding-advice)).
- FACT: Lottie is free to families, runs a free care helpline with care experts, and is paid by care homes ([leicestershire.gov.uk](https://www.leicestershire.gov.uk/popular-now/directories/information-and-support-directory/lottie-care-finder-service), [startups.co.uk](https://startups.co.uk/startups-100/2023/lottie)).
- FACT: Councils run free care-fee advice lines, e.g. Gloucestershire's Care Advice Line ([gloucestershire.gov.uk](https://gloucestershire.gov.uk/health-and-social-care/adult-social-care/paying-for-adult-social-care/where-to-get-independent-financial-advice)). Free "how long will my capital last" calculators are also common ([wecovr.com](https://wecovr.com/guides/uk-long-term-care-costs-plan-with-our-calculator/), [L&G guide](https://care.legalandgeneral.com/landg-assets/microsites/care/_documents/guides/paying-for-care-gp.pdf)).
- Paid rivals are regulated advisers and law firms (see 1).

## 4. Could a big company switch it on as a free feature? - FAIL (already free)
- FACT: Which?, Lottie, Autumna and Legal & General already give this away to generate leads (links above). Paying for Care also offers free information plus a search for local advisers (search summary, from the Staffordshire/SOLLA results). Councils give free advice under the Care Act ([which.co.uk](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/the-five-most-common-questions-people-ask-about-paying-for-care-in-later-life-aOCxV2R1LogY)).

## 5. Does it need a licence or break a site's rules? - UNCLEAR
- FACT: Introducing clients for commission on regulated insurance products, such as care-fee annuities, normally runs through FCA introducer appointed representative (IAR) status under an authorised principal ([HMRC VATINS5350](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-insurance/vatins5350), [LexisNexis](https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-gb/legal/glossary/introducer)).
- REASONABLE GUESS: A generic runway calculation is not regulated advice. But recommending a funding option (annuity, equity release, investments) would be. That pushes the useful part of the report into FCA territory, and the referral-income model needs a principal firm.

## 6. Can the first 10 buyers be reached online without a sales team? - PASS
- REASONABLE GUESS: Yes, through Mumsnet/Gransnet-style forums, Facebook dementia carer groups, Alzheimer's Society Talking Point, and SEO on "how long will my money last care home". The catch is that Which? and Lottie already dominate this search traffic (results above).

## 7. Will free AI assistants do this within 2 years? - FAIL
- OPINION: Ask a free assistant: "Mum has £180k plus a £300k house in NI, fees £1,200 a week, pension £250 a week. When does the council step in, and is the house counted?" Today it gives a usable runway and threshold answer, built on the public rules above. The only part it can't do is regulated personal advice, which this product can't do either without a licence.

## VERDICT: KILLED
Failed checks 2, 3, 4 and 7, with 5 unclear. Families do pay for care-fee help, but they pay regulated advisers and CHC law firms (fixed fees from £175 + VAT; 25% contingency fees are reported). The unregulated "runway report" is already given away free by Which? Later Life Care, Lottie, Autumna, Legal & General and council advice lines, and free AI assistants can do the maths. Referral income needs an FCA principal, and it competes with the lead-gen businesses that already own this traffic.

Biggest residual angle (OPINION): only as a lead-gen funnel run as an IAR of a SOLLA-accredited firm, which is a different and more crowded business.
