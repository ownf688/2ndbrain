## Pattern P4: entry-level squeeze, hiring funnel flooded by AI applications

### 1. Best playbook (OPINION)
- **Playbook PB1 (audience first, then product)** fits best. The squeeze makes job seekers anxious and employers drowning, and both groups gather around trusted curators. The cases in playbooks.md show audience was the biggest single head start (20 of 49 known). The user already has an HR-leaders newsletter, which is the employer side of this exact pain.
- **Playbook PB2 (ride a new capability)** is second: real-time voice AI makes cheap interview practice possible, and small operators are already showing verified revenue (below). But windows close fast and 7 of 12 AI winners' features are now free elsewhere (playbooks.md).

## Pattern P5: gas shock, households buying their way out of energy bills; landlords' EPC C by 2030

### 1. Best playbook (OPINION)
- **Playbook PB1 (audience first)** fits best. Homeowners face a big, confusing, one-off purchase (heat pump, battery, insulation) and don't trust installers. The money in this pattern goes to the trusted voice that sits between the household and the installer. Pattern P3 says trust and distribution are what stays scarce.
- **Playbook PB6 (vertical tool for a profession you live in)** fits the installer side, but only for someone who lives in the trade. The user does not, which is why O21 died.

### 3. Likely millionaire in 3 years (OPINION)
The type: **a trusted installer-network or editorial brand that controls where homeowners go next.** The clearest example is **Heat Geek** (Adam Chapman, founded 2016): trains installers, runs a network of about 1,000, 80,000 social followers, and says it took 5% of the UK heat pump market in three years (**CLAIMED**). It is **not small and not bootstrapped**: it raised a $5m seed in Feb 2024, reported as £4.2m (https://www.acrjournal.uk/heat-pumps/heat-geek-secures-4-2m-to-fuel-the-heat-pump-revolution). Right now it is doing training, certification and lead routing, so it is the gatekeeper between homeowners and installers. REASONABLE GUESS: Octopus has launched a "UK-first" solar and heat pump referral scheme (headline only, https://www.solarpowerportal.co.uk/solar-installations/octopus-unveils-uk-first-heat-pump-and-solar-referral-scheme), so audience owners may earn referral income; terms not checked.

### 4. Openings this pattern already produced, and why they died
- **O20 Solar payback checker plus leads:** free calculators (SEAI); Bark, Eco Experts, MyBuilder already sell leads at £7-40.
- **O21 Grant/grid paperwork for installers:** free forms; Payaca, Easy MCS and others; MCS umbrella schemes include it.
- **O22 Battery and tariff optimiser:** Predbat free; Octopus Intelligent Flux free; Axle pays homeowners.
- **O23 EPC C plan for landlords:** Domna about £10/property via NRLA; Eco-Landlord free and government-backed.
- **O24 Small-business energy audit:** brokers free to the customer; free regulator comparison in NI.

### 5. Raw bet ideas
1. **Home-energy benefit pack for employers**: HR policy, comms and supplier shortlist for employers offering staff help with energy upgrades (PB7, uses HR skill). REASONABLE GUESS that some employers want this; whether salary-sacrifice rules allow it for heat pumps or solar was **not checked**. No Nudge clash; low day-job clash.
2. **NI/ROI home-energy newsletter for first-time upgraders** (grants, real installed costs, reader case studies), monetised by sponsors or referral schemes (PB1). Fit: evenings, cheap. Slow; lead money crowded (O20).
3. **"Warm without the heating" line on the user's clothing label** (base layers, house wear) sold via TikTok Shop (PB3). Fit: uses an existing business; £500 sample run test. Demand link to gas prices is OPINION. No clash.
4. **Paid "second opinion on your installer quote" by a qualified heating designer, sold through the user's page** (PB7 with a partner). Fit weak: the user lacks the skill and would rely on a partner. No clash.

## Pattern P6: older people hold the wealth; April 2027 pension-IHT change makes it move

### 1. Best playbook (OPINION)
- **Playbook PB1 (audience first) feeding a regulated partner** fits best. Advice is FCA-regulated (O28, O26), so the unregulated outsider's role is the trusted explainer who owns the audience of 45 to 60 year-old adult children. The best small-scale proof found is an adviser who used YouTube as a lead engine (below).
- **Playbook PB7 (productised service)** is second, if sold to the regulated firms rather than to families.

### 3. Likely millionaire in 3 years (OPINION)
The type: **a regulated adviser who is also a creator**, using YouTube or a newsletter to pull in the families the pension-IHT change frightens, then charging advice fees on large estates. James Shack is the named current example: a 192,000-subscriber channel already covering the pension-IHT change and claiming about 120 qualified leads a month. His podcast appearance on "adviser YouTube mastery" (CLAIMED) suggests he also teaches other advisers, so treat his figures with the SELLS A COURSE caution. A non-regulated outsider is unlikely to be this millionaire; the outsider's version is selling the creator machine to advisers.

### 4. Openings this pattern already produced, and why they died
- **O25 Gift/estate record-keeper:** free trackers (HL, Fidelity); adviser tools model gifts.
- **O26 Care-fee runway report:** Which?, Lottie, Autumna, councils free; referral income needs FCA introducer route.
- **O27 Estate-admin task service:** Settld, Life Ledger, Tell Us Once free; probate reserved, and an offence in NI if unqualified.
- **O28 Equity-release leads:** FCA-regulated.
- **O29 Downsizing concierge, NI:** cheap local clearers; needs weekday on-site time.

### 5. Raw bet ideas
1. **Done-for-you video or newsletter production for small IFAs who want to be "the next James Shack"** (PB7). Regulation stays with the adviser. Fit: evenings, remote, UK-wide. Rivals not checked (the adviser-YouTube coaching market already exists, per the podcast). No Nudge or day-job clash.
2. **Employer "money at 55+" workplace sessions on the 2027 pension-IHT change** (guidance, not advice), sold to HR teams via the user's newsletter (PB1 plus PB7). Fit: uses HR network. **Flag:** guidance/advice line must be checked; a regulated co-presenter may be needed.
3. **Newsletter for adult children managing parents' money admin**, monetised by flat-fee sponsorship from authorised firms (PB1). Fit: cheap test. **Flag:** financial-promotion rules; sponsors must approve their own ads. Slow to build.
4. **Employment-law explainer pack for employers on pension and death-benefit communications ahead of April 2027** (PB6-lite). Whether employers face any new duty here was **not checked**; test only if a real obligation exists. Low clash.
