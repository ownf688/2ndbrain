# Case file: newsletters, media and creators

Seen: 2026-10-03. Stage 2, Phase 1. Research only.

**Search budget used in full (12 of 12 searches). No search error was returned, but the budget ran out before 15 cases with good evidence could be found.** Cases below the evidence bar are marked. Anything not found in a search result is marked "from memory, unverified" or "not found".

**Score at the top:**
- Cases recorded: 12 (8 that meet the money test on at least a claimed basis, plus 4 reference cases that fail the team-size, funding or money test but teach something).
- VERIFIED money figures (Stripe-verified, filed accounts, or a disclosed recorded sale price): **0**.
- Press-confirmed acquisitions where the exact price was NOT disclosed: 3 (The Neuron, Milk Road, The Hustle).
- CLAIMED or third-party ESTIMATED money figures: all the rest.
- Honest summary: newsletter money is almost never verified in public. Substack and beehiiv do not publish revenue, sale prices are usually "undisclosed", and most of the loud numbers come from beehiiv's own marketing blog (a platform with a reason to make newsletters look lucrative) or from people who sell newsletter courses.

Jargon: CPM = price an advertiser pays per 1,000 readers reached. ARR = annual recurring revenue. "Boosts" = beehiiv's paid-referral network where one newsletter pays another per new subscriber.

- **Time to first sale / to £1m:** started 2018 (older start). Time to first sale: not found. Time to $1m: not found.
- **First 100 customers:** not found in these searches.
- **Head start:** tech worker himself (wrote for his own peers); timing before the newsletter boom.

## Patterns in this type (OPINION)

- **Almost nothing is verified.** 0 of 12 cases have a Stripe-verified, filed-accounts or disclosed sale-price figure. 3 of 12 have a press-confirmed sale with the price hidden. Any plan built on "newsletters make £1m" rests on platform marketing and course sellers.
- **The audience came first.** 6 of 9 cases with known backgrounds started with an existing audience or insider status in the readers' profession (Milk Road, Lenny, Pragmatic Engineer, TLDR, The Hustle, Superhuman AI). The user has this for HR leaders.
- **Free plus ads is the main route to £1m; paid subscriptions reach it only for star practitioners.** 6 of 8 qualifying cases are free, ad-funded. Only 2 (Lenny, Pragmatic Engineer) are paid, and both are ex-big-tech insiders writing for big-salary professions.
- **The money test is usually met by the sale, not the sales.** The Neuron sold for "seven figures" on about $825K revenue in 18 months. For a small B2B list, an exit to a vendor in the niche (HR tech, payroll, ATS) is the more realistic £1m path than yearly revenue. Rule of thumb seen: 2x to 4x annual profit.
- **Most rode a wave.** 5 of 12 rode a hot topic (AI x4, crypto x1). HR has no equal wave, except AI-in-HR and UK employment law change (Employment Rights Act roll-out), which could act as a smaller one.
- **The team-of-5 cap breaks as they grow.** 4 of 12 (TLDR, Superhuman, Rundown, Hustle) passed 5 staff once revenue crossed a few million. Solo stays solo only on the paid-writer model.
- **HR niche evidence is thin.** No HR, legal or finance newsletter found with a public £1m figure. The best HR lists sit at 30,000 to 40,000 readers, and a funded rival (Workweek) already chases HR-leader sponsors. Matt Brown's $200K on 2,000 readers suggests the realistic small-niche ceiling is six figures unless paired with events, data products or a sale.
