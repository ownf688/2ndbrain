# Case file: real-world businesses run remotely with subcontractors

Type: cleaning, junk removal, pressure washing, vending, self storage, laundromats, serviced accommodation, local lead-gen, "boring business" buys.
Date seen for all links: 2026-10-03. Search budget: all 12 searches used (no error returned). Most sources were seen in search results only, not checked at the original page.

**Count: 18 cases logged. 11 claim to clear the £1m/$1m bar; 7 are near misses or excluded, kept for context. VERIFIED: 0. CLAIMED: 18.**

Honest headline: this type has almost no verified figures. There is no TrustMRR-style payment-verified source for trade businesses. The evidence is podcasts, vendor blogs (software firms marketing to cleaners), broker listings and press. Companies House checks for the UK cases were not done (budget). A large share of the loudest voices sell a course or coaching.

Jargon: "subcontractor" = self-employed worker paid per job, not an employee. "Semi-absentee" = owner works a few hours a week, a manager runs daily work. "Rank and rent" = build a local website that ranks on Google, then rent its leads to a tradesperson.

## Patterns in this type (OPINION)

- **Zero verified figures in 18 cases.** Every number is a podcast, vendor blog, broker listing or press claim. Software vendors (BookingKoala, Jobber) and course sellers are the main publishers of success stories. At least 5 of 18 cases sell a course, coaching, franchise or consulting (Kircher, Parekh, Robinson, Geiman, plus the rank-and-rent material).
- **Remote cleaning is the only sub-type with a real "solo owner plus subcontractors" track record.** 3 of the 11 qualifying cases (1, 2, 3) are remote cleaning, and 4 of the 7 near misses are too. The usual first-year number is $120k to $150k; $1M takes about 4 to 5 years.
- **Owner time is rarely stated, and where it is low, the business has a manager or employees.** Only 4 cases give hours: under 8 hours a month (junk listing), part time (junk listing), 4 hours a week and 4 to 5 hours a week (both vending, both below the bar). The $1M+ firms with low owner time (laundromats, junk removal, storage) all carry staff, so they break the 5-person rule.
- **Customers come with the asset or from paid local search.** In buy-in cases (laundromat, storage, junk listings: 4 of 11) the first 100 customers were inherited. In built cases, the channel was not stated precisely in any of the 11; the implied channel is Google local search, ads and booking software.
- **Head start is usually a day job, not an audience.** 3 of 11 qualifying cases mention a prior job that funded or seeded the business (Kircher at Redfin, Parekh corporate, Menz lineman); none relied on an existing online audience.
- **Capital splits the type in two.** Cleaning, pressure washing and lead-gen start under $5k (Hodges under $2,000, Marcus $93). Storage, laundromats and serviced accommodation need six or seven figures and bank debt.
- **Waves ridden:** Airbnb growth (MaidThis, GuestReady), remote-management tech (storage), cheap booking/scheduling software (remote cleaning), post-2020 comfort with running a local firm from elsewhere. UK rule changes (holiday-let tax from April 2025) work against new serviced-accommodation entrants.
- **Fit for a Belfast builder with a day job (OPINION):** remote cleaning in a different UK town is the only one with a proven solo path, but the moat is ad skill, reviews and cleaner retention, not software. Weather hurts pressure washing in Northern Ireland. The user's HR and employment-law skill is a genuine edge on the subcontractor-status risk that most US playbooks ignore (UK employment-status rules, worker versus self-employed, make "subcontracted cleaners" legally riskier than in the US).
