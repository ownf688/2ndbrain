# Case file: courses, communities and paid information

Type: cohort courses, paid communities, templates, paid newsletters, data products, paid job boards, salary/benchmark reports.
Date seen for all links: 2026-10-03. Searches used: 12 of 12 (budget reached, no limit error).

**Count at the top:** 19 entries. 10 meet the £1m/$1m bar on the evidence found. Of those, **2 VERIFIED** (ShipFast via TrustMRR-sourced figure, Backlinko via press-confirmed acquisition) and **8 CLAIMED**. A further 9 are near-misses, unconfirmed or excluded, listed so the gaps are visible. All figures are US dollars unless stated.

Method note: most pages were not fetched. Where a figure is cited it was "seen in search result, not checked at the original page" unless said otherwise. Jargon: MRR = monthly recurring revenue; cohort course = a live course where a group starts and finishes together; boilerplate = ready-made starter code.

## A. Cases that meet the bar

### 1. The Pragmatic Engineer (Gergely Orosz) - paid newsletter for software engineers
- Who/team: Gergely Orosz, solo writer (later small helpers; exact team size not found). Sells a paid newsletter on engineering careers, big-tech practice and industry news to working engineers and engineering managers. $15/month or $150/year (2026), group and regional discounts.
- Sales/profit: "more than $1.5 million in annual revenue" per one source; another estimate says up to $17.9m. **CLAIMED/third-party estimate.** No sponsorships or ads; almost all revenue is subscriptions. Passed 1 million subscribers (free + paid) in 2025; paid share "single digit percentage". Profit not found. Bar met: yearly sales.
- Funding: none found (bootstrapped).
- Time to first sale: paid newsletter launched 2021; time to first sale not found. Time to $1m not found.
- First 100 customers: not found exactly. Substack "Grow series" interview covers early growth (not checked).
- Head start: ex-engineering manager at Uber, Skype/Microsoft; existing blog and audience before launch.
- Big change ridden: remote work and the 2021-22 tech hiring boom, then layoffs (engineers wanting inside information); Substack paid subscriptions.
- Failed attempts before: not found.
- Copy difficulty (OPINION): hard. The product is one person's rare insider network and credibility. Easy to start, hard to reach paid scale without the reputation.
- Links: https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/2021-review ; https://on.substack.com/p/grow-series-21-gergely-orosz ; https://kyuhwangcho.substack.com/p/top-earners-on-substack (publication dates not shown in results).

## Patterns in this type (OPINION)

- **Audience first, product second.** 9 of the 10 qualifying cases had a large audience before the paid product (YouTube, Twitter/X, blog, open-source users or a free newsletter). The only one unclear is We Work Remotely, where the owner was not found.
- **Verified money is rare in this type.** Only 2 of 10 qualifying cases have verified figures, and both are oddities (a TrustMRR-listed code template; a press-confirmed acquisition with a price range). Everything else rests on founder posts or third-party estimates.
- **"Make money" sellers are heavily represented.** 4 of 10 qualifying cases sell partly to people who want to make money online (ShipFast/CodeFast, Ship 30, Everything Marketplaces, and Backlinko to a lesser degree). Professional know-how to professionals is the cleaner subset: Pragmatic Engineer, Lenny's, Refactoring UI/Tailwind UI.
- **AI is cutting both ways.** 3 cases show AI eroding paid information (Josh Comeau's falling launch, Tailwind downsizing, Notion/boilerplate templates now free via AI) while 1 shows AI creating demand (AI Evals selling out at $2k-5k). Lesson: sell the newest professional skill gap, not reference material AI can reproduce.
- **Paid newsletters for one profession are the strongest recent route.** 2 of 10 (Pragmatic Engineer, Lenny's) cleared $1m+ a year with no ads, both built on the founder's insider seniority in that profession. That is directly the shape of Owen's HR-leaders newsletter.
- **HR, recruiting and legal info products at $1m+ by tiny teams were not found** in 12 searches. The closest are HR360 ($278k) and recruiter communities with no revenue figures. Either the gap is real or these businesses do not publish numbers (UK ones could be checked on Companies House).
- **Job boards make money from employers; the big ones are older (2011-2015) and saturated.** 2 of 10 qualifying cases are job boards, both pre-2021 and remote-focused. A niche profession board is still possible but ceilings look like $60k/month at best in recent cases found.
- **Recency:** 0 of 10 qualifying cases started in the last 24 months; most started 2018-2021. Recent wins in this type are hard to find with verified numbers.
