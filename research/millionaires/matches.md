## Pattern P2: dear, scarce low-paid labour

### 1. Best playbooks: PB7 (productised service, then productise), with PB4 (high-ticket recruiting) for scarce roles
OPINION. Firms in this pattern buy anything that removes a staff hour or fills a hard role. PB7 sells a finished job at a fixed price, which is what a firm cutting staff wants; playbooks.md cases (Bookkeeping for Painters, CFOAF) niche to one trade. PB4 fits because the July 2025 visa rule (care worker sponsorship closed to new overseas recruits from 22 Jul 2025, FACT, search result: https://shiftcare.com/uk/blog/after-the-overseas-recruitment-ban-how-uk-care-providers-can-build-a-domestic-workforce) makes domestic hires scarce, and scarcity is what lets a recruiter charge a big fee per hire.

### 2. Small operators riding it today
**Mostly not found.** My searches did not surface a named solo or tiny UK operator in this pattern with a price and traction figure. What I did find:
- Reqo, AI receptionist at £99/month (from killed.md O6; size and traction not found).
- Elyos AI, AI agents answering calls and booking jobs for trades: raised $13m (FACT, search result via cfotech.co.uk tag page), so **not small**; it shows where the funded money is going.
- Analogues (playbooks.md, CLAIMED, not confirmed as riding this pattern): Bookkeeping for Painters, Invenio, Candid Talent.

OPINION: the absence is itself a signal. Tiny operators here sell via Fiverr or local word of mouth and do not publish; funded firms (Elyos) take the software side.

### 3. Likely millionaire in 3 years (OPINION)
A **niche recruiter or staffing operator for domiciliary care or hospitality groups** who builds a local candidate pool the sector used to source abroad, paid per hire or per filled shift (PB4). Skills for Care put vacancies at about 152,000 in 2023/24 with 28-35% turnover (FACT, search result, same ShiftCare page). Named operator: not found. What sets them up now: owning a candidate community (career-changers, returners) and a referral scheme while providers are still reacting. This needs weekday phone time and possibly agency rules, so it is a poor fit for the user.

### 4. Openings this pattern already produced, and why they died
O6 (AI receptionist: 8+ priced rivals, booking platforms bundle voice AI), O8 (pub rota: £1-3 per employee tools, Square free), O9 (apprenticeship admin: cheap incumbents), O10 (nursery billing: standard in 7+ systems), O15 (AI workflow set-up: Fiverr from $20, free NI AICC help), O16 (shortlisting: £349-995 fixed-fee rivals, free Indeed AI), O29, O32, O33 (labour-heavy, fail fit). **Common cause:** generic hour-saving software is a commodity; labour-heavy services break the day-job limit.

### 5. Raw bet ideas
1. **Retention diagnostic for small care providers** (PB7): exit-interview and 90-day turnover analysis, fixed £495, delivered async from their data. Sells reduced hiring, not software. Fit: async, under £500 test (10 prospects); no licence. Check: care managers buy in weekday hours.
2. **"Hire locally now" referral-scheme set-up for hospitality groups of 3-10 sites** (PB7): design plus a Slack/WhatsApp feedback loop reused from Nudge. Fit: evenings build, outreach by email; risk of drifting into O16 territory.
3. **Paid-per-hire niche recruiting in a sector far from payments** (PB4). Highest money, worst fit: day-job conflict and weekday calls. List only if employer consents.
