**Law: is this allowed without a licence?**
- Employer side is not FCA claims-management work. FCA activity 89M covers "advising a claimant or potential claimant... investigating a claim and representing a claimant" ([FCA glossary](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/glossary/G3569a.html)). CIPD: "You do not need FCA authorisation if you are advising employers on claims against them" ([cipd.org](https://www.cipd.org/uk/the-people-profession/careers/routes-into-profession/claims-management/)). Employee-side help for a fee is regulated. **Confirmed for GB.** Whether the FCA regime reaches NI: not checked (UNCLEAR).
- Mazur (2025, "conduct of litigation" reserved under the Legal Services Act 2007): the summaries found say employment tribunals sit among forums where the restriction does not bite because of tribunal rules ([Law Society](https://www.lawsociety.org.uk/topics/civil-litigation/10-considerations-when-conducting-litigation-after-mazur), [Beale & Co](https://beale-law.com/article/after-mazur-how-the-law-society-cilex-and-lsb-are-redrawing-the-lines-on-litigation-conduct/)). ## M1. Fixed-fee ET3 "grounds of resistance" for uninsured small employers (GB, NI as add-on)
**One line:** a small employer uploads the ET1 and their papers; within 5 working days they get a drafted ET3 and grounds of resistance plus a one-page risk note, for about £495-795.
- **Who pays:** owner-managers of 5-50 staff firms with no HR retainer and no legal expenses insurance, or whose insurer declined the claim.
- **Proof of pay:** law firms sell the same deliverable at £1,000-4,500 (above). Direct sellers below £1,000: not found.
- **Price gap and buyers in it:** the law-firm floor is about £1,000-1,750 against free DIY. A £495-795 price sits at roughly half the floor, not a clear 3x gap against the typical £1,500-3,500 + VAT (3x holds only against the £2,000+ quotes). Buyers asking for this middle tier: **not found.**
- **Forcing clock:** 28 days from the claim, default judgment if missed. Rising receipts (+28% to +58% year on year), 6-month time limit from 1 Oct 2026.
- **Comparable revenue:** not found.
- **First sale in 2 weeks for £500 or less:** plausible in theory (service, Stripe link, landing page, Google Ads on "ET3 response"), but the buyer finds out only when a claim lands, so paid search or referrals from accountants are the only routes. Not tested.
- **User assets:** GB/NI employment law, People leadership, build skill (intake form plus an AI first-draft pipeline the user reviews).
- **Evidence against:** no priced middle-tier sellers and no buyer posts found; Peninsula/WorkNest/Citation bundle defence into retainers; free AI drafts a passable ET3; the user is not a solicitor, so no privilege and possible PI exposure; a weak ET3 can hurt a defence.

## M2. Written tribunal prep pack for self-representing employers (after the ET3)
**One line:** fixed-fee written work once a case is listed: chronology, document list and bundle index, draft witness statements, counter-schedule of loss, and a hearing script for the owner to read. The employer attends the hearing themselves.
- **Who pays:** the same uninsured small employers, one stage later.
- **Proof of pay:** law firms price "preparation for hearing" in the same SRA menus (Trowers, Fosters, Mackrell PDFs above); exact stage prices were not extracted. Direct sellers of a prep-only pack: not found.
- **Price gap:** full defence to hearing commonly runs well beyond the ET3 stage (figures not extracted this session). Buyers in between: not found.
- **Forcing clock:** case management orders set dated deadlines for documents and witness statements. Listings run into 2027-28 because of backlog ([Herrington Carmichael](https://herrington-carmichael.com/insights/employment/employment-tribunals-under-strain/)), so the clock is real but slow.
- **Comparable revenue:** not found.
- **First sale in 2 weeks:** unlikely from a cold start; best sold as a follow-on to M1 buyers.
- **User assets:** employment law, build skill (bundle and chronology tooling).
- **Evidence against:** the work is large and varied (20-60 hours a case is plausible, not checked), so it does not fit evenings at a fixed fee; the employer still has to perform at a weekday hearing.

## M3. Employer DSAR response desk for employment disputes (1-month clock)

- **Proof of pay:** law firms and data-protection consultancies sell exactly this: Ashtons Legal (fixed-fee retainer), Prettys, Osborne Clarke, Squire Patton Boggs, The DPO Centre (per-DSAR fixed fees, "pay as you go for up to about 20 requests a year") ([Ashtons](https://ashtonslegal.co.uk/your-business/employment-law-for-employers/data-subject-access-request-dsar), [DPO Centre](https://www.dpocentre.com/services/dsar-response-service/), [Osborne Clarke PDF](https://www.osborneclarke.com/system/files/documents/26/04/23/DSAR-Management-Flyer%28160754552.4%29-%281%29.pdf)). **Prices not found** in the snippets.

## M4. Independent investigation report, written up from evening interviews
**One line:** a fixed-fee independent grievance or disciplinary investigation: scope, video interviews in the evening, evidence review, and a findings report in 2-3 weeks, at about £1,500-2,500.
- **Who pays:** employers where the usual manager is conflicted or the matter is sensitive; public bodies and charities buy it often.
- **Proof of pay:** strong. FOI disclosures show employers paying: a specialist workplace practice £6,801 + VAT for 68.25 hours (2023/24), law firms £4,422-9,300 per investigation ([Lincolnshire CC FOI](https://www.lincolnshire.gov.uk/directory-record/82470/the-use-of-external-or-independent-investigators-for-workplace-matters), [ONS FOI](https://cy.ons.gov.uk/aboutus/transparencyandgovernance/freedomofinformationfoi/useofexternalorindependentinvestigatorsforinternalworkplacematters)). Solo investigators sell from £100/hour with a fixed fee after scoping ([Enterprise Nation, Alex Mihai](https://www.enterprisenation.com/get-connected/workplace-investigations-1/)). The charity YESS Law offers "affordable fixed fees" ([yesslaw.org.uk](https://www.yesslaw.org.uk/employers/workplace-investigations)).
- **Price gap and buyers in it:** law firms at £4,400-9,300 against solo investigators at about £100/hour. A £1,500-2,500 fixed price is a 2-6x gap below law firms, but solo investigators and a charity already sit in that middle. So the middle is served, not empty.
- **Forcing clock:** no statutory date. The Acas Code requires action "without unreasonable delay", and a tribunal can raise awards by up to 25% for unreasonable failure to follow it (general law, not re-verified this session). Grievances rise with the new 6-month time limit (inference).
- **Comparable revenue:** not found (no accounts checked).
- **First sale in 2 weeks:** possible via Enterprise Nation and HR-consultant subcontracting, not tested.
- **User assets:** People leadership (investigations are routine HR work), employment law.
- **Evidence against:** witnesses work weekdays, so evening interviews may not always be possible (fit risk); the middle is already served (check 3 risk); a Head of People doing investigations for other firms raises profile and conflict questions.

- **Proof of pay:** law firms sell "advice on early conciliation" inside hourly or SRA menus (not extracted); a firm sells employer-side settlement agreement drafting at £750 + VAT (snippet, firm not named). Direct sellers of this exact assessment: not found.
- **Price gap, buyers in between:** not found.
- **Forcing clock:** the early-conciliation period is fixed (6 weeks under current rules; any ERA 2025 extension not verified this session), and an ET1 follows if it fails. Volumes: Acas EC notifications not found this round.
- **Comparable revenue:** not found.
