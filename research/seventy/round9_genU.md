## Licence check (applies to every idea)

- **GB and NI: no licence.** The REC says most UK agencies need no licence or registration; NI's rules are the Employment (Miscellaneous Provisions) (NI) Order 1981 and the Conduct of Employment Agencies and Employment Businesses Regulations (NI) 2005 ([REC](https://www.rec.uk.com/recruiters/legal/legal-guide/licences-and-registration/do-i-need-licence-or-other-registration-run-employment-agency-or-business); [DfE NI guidance](https://www.economy-ni.gov.uk/publications/guidance-conduct-employment-agencies-and-employment-business-regulations); dates not shown). FACT: no registration, but the NI Employment Agency Inspectorate can inspect records, and the regulations govern fees (no charging candidates), terms agreed with hirers, and checks on candidates ([LRA](https://www.lra.org.uk/legislation/no395-conduct-employment-agencies-and-employment-businesses-regulations-northern); [DfE EAI](https://economy-ni.gov.uk/eai)). Practical cost: written terms of business with each client and candidate-suitability records. GLAA licensing applies only to labour-supply sectors (agriculture, shellfish, food processing), not to these niches (from REC context; not separately searched).
- **Republic of Ireland: licence needed.** An agency with premises in the State must hold a licence under the Employment Agency Act 1971; €500 a year, two character references and Garda vetting; trading without one is an offence (fine up to €2,500 plus €1,000 a day) ([WRC](https://www.workplacerelations.ie/en/what_you_should_know/employer-obligations/employment-agencies); [Irish Statute Book](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/1971/act/27); dates not shown). Whether a Belfast-based agency placing into ROI clients needs one is **UNCLEAR** (the trigger is premises in the State). OPINION: treat ROI work as out of scope for the test, which keeps the "no licence" rule intact.
- **Contractor (interim) supply** turns an agency into an "employment business" with pay, IR35 and liability duties. OPINION: keep every idea to permanent placement or retainer only.

## Fee and economics baseline

- Contingency fees: 15-25% of first-year salary, 20% the usual figure; 20% "on a multiple agency basis" is common in the UK ([Aaron Wallis FAQ](https://www.aaronwallis.co.uk/faq-recruitment-costs.aspx); [Pin freelance guide](https://www.pin.com/blog/freelance-recruiter-guide/); dates not shown). Retained and specialist search 25-33%; AI engineering at 30%+ on search-led work ([Acceler8 Talent, 2026](https://www.acceler8talent.com/resources/blog/ai-recruitment-agency-fees-to-place-a-head-of-ai-in-2026/)).
- AI sourcing tools fit the £500 test: Pin Solo $99/month, Professional $135/user/month; Juicebox Starter $99-119/month, agents $199/agent/month ([Pin vs Juicebox](https://ghost.pin.com/pin-vs-juicebox/); [Noon on Juicebox pricing 2026](https://www.noon.ai/blog/articles/249-juicebox-pricing-2026)). FACT (vendor pages).
- Comparables (all CLAIMED, none VERIFIED): Candid Talent (Jon Woolley), £112k year one at about 25 hours a week, $500k year two ([RAG podcast](https://podcastaddict.com/episode/https%3A%2F%2Fepisodes.captivate.fm%2Fepisode%2F01db5a14-e022-4517-b74e-04c3ef09d9a1.mp3&podcastId=3022362)); Invenio Talent $1.3m year one, four people; Pin's unnamed solo recruiter "$1m billings in four months" (vendor blog, [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/solo-recruiter-ai-billings/)).
- **Time to cash (applies to all):** a placement is paid after the candidate starts, usually after a 1-3 month notice period, and often with a rebate or guarantee window (Paraform pays in instalments across a 90-day guarantee, [HeroHunt](https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/paraform-pricing-alternatives-2026/)). OPINION: a signed fee agreement or accepted offer inside 2 weeks is possible; cash inside 2 weeks almost never is. Only a retainer (U7) or an upfront search fee can be cash inside the test.
