- **Rivals and gap:** thousands of experts on each network; the gap is narrow: a current, hands-on buyer of ATS/HRIS/interview tools at a VC-backed scale-up. Not found: any data on how many HR-tech expert calls are requested.
- **Timing:** REASONABLE GUESS: AI-in-HR funding wave keeps investor research demand up; no dated trigger.
- **Comparable earnings:** not found (no verified solo earnings from expert calls).
- **First paid engagement in 2 weeks/£500:** sign-up is free and fast; whether a call is offered inside 2 weeks is UNCLEAR (depends on project flow). Cost £0.
- **Assets used:** hiring/People leadership, hands-on HR-tech buying, Claude Code build experience (AI-in-HR views).

- **Evidence against:** low ticket per call; volume outside the user's control; calls are often scheduled in US/UK business hours (REASONABLE GUESS); size of prize likely under £10-20k/yr.

- **Rivals and gap:** law firms do employment legal DD; Big Four/RSM do HR DD for larger deals ([RSM](https://www.rsm.global/japan/shiodome/en/services/transaction-advisory/due-diligence)); AIHR publishes free checklists ([AIHR](https://www.aihr.com/blog/hr-due-diligence/)). Possible gap: deals too small for a Big Four HR workstream, where the lawyer covers contracts but nobody assesses retention, manager quality and people cost. Not proven by any cited buyer complaint.

- **Conflicts:** JOB low (not fintech targets; avoid payments companies). NUDGE none. Legal: commenting on legal risk is not a reserved activity in GB, but liability for a missed issue suggests PI insurance (cost not found); report must not present as legal advice.
- **Evidence against:** buyers may treat it as "the lawyer already covers employment"; free checklists plus AI produce a passable list; deal flow is lumpy; DD windows are short and can need daytime calls with sellers.

## Generator's notes (OPINION, not a score)
- Strongest on proof of pay: F6 (NED fees, CLAIMED) and F1 (expert calls, CLAIMED), but both fail on speed or ticket size or fit.
- Strongest on fit and use of assets: F2 and F4 (evening document work, law plus data tooling, no fintech clash if payments targets are excluded). Both lack a UK priced seller of the exact offer and any dated trigger; both are exposed to "the lawyer already does it" and free checklists plus AI.
- Pattern from this angle: where buyers clearly pay for a senior human (DD, NED, assessment), the work is either daytime, slow to land, or already bundled into a lawyer's or search firm's fee. The scarce input is trust, which a stranger without a track record of the exact deliverable does not yet have.
