Seen 2026-10-04. Source: `../round5_genN.md`. 10 web searches. Impact assessment PDF fetch blocked (proxy 403); its facts come from search-result text.

## Facts settled
- **Date and process (FACT):** Code and Regulations in force 30 Oct 2026. Applies to employers with 21+ workers, counted across associated employers in GB. Employer has 15 working days to respond using a **standard form in the Code**, then 25 working days to negotiate; CAC decides if no agreement, referral by day 55 (extendable to 70) ([Lewis Silkin, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/09/21/trade-union-access-rights-to-the-workplace); [BTO](https://bto.co.uk/blog/trade-unions-and-the-new-right-of-access-what-employers-need-to-know/); [Withers](https://www.withersworldwide.com/en-gb/insight/read/union-access-to-uk-workplaces-%E2%80%93-a-significant-new-right-comes-into-effect-on-30-october-2026)).
- **Penalties (FACT):** for breaching an access agreement/order, up to £75,000 first breach, £150,000 second, £500,000 repeated; may be published ([Mondaq/BTO](https://www.mondaq.com/trade-unions-and-the-new-right-of-access-what-employers-need-to-know/1835936); [Doyle Clayton](https://doyleclayton.co.uk/employers-beware-new-trade-union-access-rights-from-october-2026-update-and-faqs/)). No penalty found for a late or poor 15-day response itself; the risk is a CAC order.
- **Volumes (FACT, damaging):** the final impact assessment (DBT, 6 Jul 2026) expects "only a small proportion of employers" affected each year, "principally larger private sector employers", and voluntary agreements to stay unions' preferred route ([Pinsent Masons Out-Law](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/en-gb/out-law/analysis/british-employers-prepare-receive-era-trade-union-access-requests); [IA PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a4b9af3d200ca05e289e5e7/trade-union-statutory-access-to-workplaces-final-impact-assessment.pdf)). ## Kill reasoning
1. The response is a **government standard form**. Filling it, with the Code's model terms beside it, is a 1-hour job a free assistant can help with (checks 2, 7).
2. The government expects requests to hit mainly **larger** private employers, so the 21-250 segment has few buyers, and the ones who get a request mostly already hold a Peninsula-type retainer or a law firm (checks 1, 4, OPINION).
3. The "handling" half means negotiation meetings with union officials inside a 25-working-day window, which happen in **weekday working hours** (REASONABLE GUESS), so it breaks the evenings-only limit (check 8).
4. **Conflict (OPINION):** a named Head of People at a payments firm publicly selling "how to respond to unions" draws union and press attention to the employer. Repeats the round 1 G8 cut (crowding + conflict); the new dated clock is outweighed by the small, large-employer volume. GB only.
