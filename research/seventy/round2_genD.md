## Generator's notes (OPINION, not a score)
- **Best evidence of payment on a seam:** D1 (Legal Island's priced NI subscription shows NI HR people pay annually for law content) and D3 (Belfast fixed fees for contracts and handbooks). Neither shows anyone paying for the *cross-jurisdiction* product itself.
- **Strongest dated trigger:** D4 (Greece 1 Nov 2026), but it uses the user's recruiting skill, not their law expertise, and ATS vendors can absorb it.
- **Recurring problem across all nine:** buyer counts at the seam are not found (only commuters, 18,318, are counted). This confirms round 1's lesson 3: seams are under-served because they are small, which caps "size of prize" and slows first sale.
- **Free-help pattern repeats** for D2, D3 and D6 (InterTradeIreland, Invest NI, ECNI, Peninsula free NI templates).
- **New evidence relevant to R8:** Peninsula's free NI contracts and handbooks and the Invest NI free handbook count against R8's "how empty" score; Belfast fixed-fee prices (£400-1,200, CLAIMED) and Legal Island (£295-895/yr) support its "proof of pay" as a close substitute. The SSP-applies-to-NI detail shows the divergence is not a simple "GB only" switch, which is the argument for a maintained register (D1) rather than a one-off split.
