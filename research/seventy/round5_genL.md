## L1. Remote independent workplace investigator (grievance, disciplinary, whistleblowing), evidence gathered by video, report written evenings

**One line:** fixed-scope independent investigations for boards and owner-managers who cannot investigate their own senior people.

- **Who pays:** small and mid employers, charities, school boards and trusts, councils (via frameworks).
- **Proof of pay:** NGA charges schools £899/day (members) or £995/day (others), most cases 2-4 days, so about £1,800-4,000 a case (NGA rate card 2026-27: https://www.nga.org.uk/media/ztbgk50i/nga-consultancy-independent-hr-investigations-26-27.pdf). WorkNest sells investigations, hearings and appeals (https://worknest.com/employment-law-hr/hr-consultancy/investigations-hearings-and-appeals/), price not shown. Councils pay external investigators (FOI answers, S5). Scottish Government paid £360/day in 2021 (old, low anchor). So 2 priced sellers found, plus public spend; 3rd priced seller not found.
- **Scarcity ("empty"):** S3, S4, S5. Supply is short because investigators need to be credible, independent and trained, and most are HR consultancies' associates or law firms. No evidence of waiting lists or turned-away buyers was found. Rivals clearly exist (NGA, WorkNest, law firms, Safecall), so "empty" is about thin supply at the small-buyer end, not no rivals.
- **Timing:** steady demand; possible lift from the Employment Rights Act (6-month unfair dismissal qualifying period from 1 Jan 2027, as recorded in earlier rounds, B3), which makes fair process matter for more staff. Not re-verified here. No dated trigger forces *an investigation* purchase.
- **Comparable revenue:** not found (no solo investigator revenue VERIFIED or CLAIMED).
- **First sale in 2 weeks/£500:** possible only through warm network (a referral from an HR consultancy or law firm needing a spare investigator). Cold: unlikely in 2 weeks.
- **User assets:** UK/NI employment law, People leadership seniority. Build skill could speed transcript-to-report (interview notes, chronology).
- **Conflicts:** day job: outside paid HR work for other employers needs contract check; reputational risk if a case goes to tribunal and the user is a witness (tribunal hearings are weekdays). Nudge: none.
- **Evidence against:** witness interviews normally happen in work hours; tribunal attendance is weekday; NI has the LRA, which offers free arbitration and independent appeal panels (https://www.lra.org.uk/node/799), weakening NI demand; insurance (PI) cost not checked.

## L2. Associate investigator / hearing chair for firms that already hold the demand (overflow capacity)

**One line:** sell evenings-and-weekends capacity to HR consultancies, law firms and frameworks that have more investigations than investigators.

- **Who pays:** HR consultancies, employment law firms, NGA-type bodies, framework holders (ICGS).
- **Proof of pay:** same as L1; associates are typically paid a share of a £900-1,000/day end price (associate rate not found).
- **Scarcity:** S4 (seller says demand is rising), S5 (framework procurement for investigators). Not found: an associate-recruitment post that says "we cannot find investigators".
- **Timing:** steady.
- **Comparable revenue:** not found.
- **First sale:** depends on being accepted onto a panel; frameworks run on procurement timelines (months). Unlikely in 2 weeks.
- **User assets:** seniority, employment law.
- **Conflicts:** as L1, plus the panel firm controls scheduling (daytime).
- **Evidence against:** this is closer to a part-time job than a business; prize capped by hours; daytime scheduling is the norm.

## L3. "Adjustments while you wait": interim reasonable-adjustments plan for employers whose staff are stuck in the Access to Work queue

**One line:** a fixed-fee, HR-led adjustments plan, manager guide and review schedule so an employer meets its Equality Act duty during the 4-9 month Access to Work wait.

- **Who pays:** employers (HR or owner) with a disabled or neurodivergent employee waiting on Access to Work.
- **Proof of pay:** employers already buy private workplace needs assessments (Exceptional Individuals: https://exceptionalindividuals.com/employers/workplace-needs-assessments/; "implementation support" add-on £500, per search snippet); AXA and Zurich bundle neurodiversity assessments into health cover (https://www.axahealth.co.uk/business/products/corporate-health-insurance/neurodiversity-assessment-and-support/). Full assessment prices not found. This is a close substitute, not the same offer.
- **Scarcity:** S6 is the strongest scarcity signal found in this round: a government service whose queue tripled in 4 years. A Training Industry piece advises HR to "consider commissioning private assessments" because waiting is "not a defence" (https://trainingmag.com/?p=1005348). Private assessor waiting lists: not found.
- **Timing:** live wave with a stated end: DWP expects the backlog cleared by end Sep 2027 (S6). That is a window of about 12 months, after which demand likely falls.
- **Comparable revenue:** not found.
- **First sale:** plausible via HR network if priced at £300-600; untested.
- **User assets:** UK/NI employment law (Equality Act / NI disability law differs: NI uses the Disability Discrimination Act 1995, a real seam), People leadership.
- **Conflicts:** low with day job and Nudge.
- **Evidence against:** clinical or assistive-tech assessment needs skills the user lacks (assessors are often occupational therapists or specialist assessors); insurers giving it free in health cover (Zurich, AXA); the employee's own assessment through Access to Work still happens eventually; Acas and Scope give adjustment guidance free (https://business.scope.org.uk/?p=7809). Closely related to O3-style "free template" kills unless the value is live judgement on the case.

## L4. Claude Code specialist on Fiverr/Upwork for UK small-business automations (not HR, not fintech)

**One line:** fixed-price Claude Code builds (internal tools, scripts, agents, migrations) sold on the marketplaces where demand is outrunning qualified sellers.

- **Who pays:** small businesses and startups posting jobs on Fiverr and Upwork.
- **Proof of pay:** claimed rates $95-175/hour, code-review gigs $150-400, refactors $1.5k-5k, migrations $3k-12k (betonai.net / UpHunt articles, 2026, CLAIMED, vendor content); MentorCruise lists 53+ Claude Code experts for hire (Jul 2026: https://mentorcruise.com/experts/claudecode/); an Upwork job "WordPress Editor for Claude Code" exists (https://www.upwork.com/jobs/~022066173801508301565).
- **Scarcity:** S1 (938% demand rise), S2 (Claude top-requested model; small qualified pool, CLAIMED). Fiverr frames it as businesses adopting "faster than they can train their existing workforce".
- **Must not repeat O15/R5:** O15 died because Fiverr gigs start at $20 and there is no moat; R5 because buyers build it themselves. **New evidence:** the 938% demand surge (Jun 2026) is after those kills. It does not remove the $20 floor or the moat problem; it only shows demand growing faster than supply for now.
- **Timing:** new wave, paid demand first visible within the last 12 months (Nov 2025-Apr 2026 data).
- **Comparable revenue:** "$2K-$10K/month" for a solo consultant (CLAIMED, blog, no named operator). VERIFIED: not found.
- **First sale in 2 weeks/£500:** feasible in principle (marketplace jobs are posted daily), but new profiles with no reviews win less.
- **User assets:** build skill (21 tools); People/HR knowledge only if niched to HR buyers (which repeats R1-R5 and J3 day-job supplier conflict).
- **Conflicts:** day job: avoid payments and HR-tech clients; outside-work clause to check. Nudge: none if not HR.
- **Evidence against:** marketplaces race to the bottom; Fiverr/Upwork fees (about 20% on Fiverr, not re-checked); the "scarcity" is search growth, which supply catches fast (lesson from round 4: waves are supplied within months).

## L5. Specialist clerk for governing-board HR panels (staff dismissal, grievance, capability and appeal committees), remote, evenings

**One line:** clerk and procedural adviser for the HR-heavy panels school and academy boards must convene, where general clerks are scarce and HR procedure errors cost tribunals.

- **Who pays:** maintained schools, academy trusts, possibly charities; often via council or trust SLAs.
- **Proof of pay:** remote clerking sold at £280 (committee), £355 (full board), £410 (trust board) per meeting by Lincolnshire (https://www.lincolnshire.gov.uk/directory-record/66287/clerking); Royal Greenwich £238-264 per extra meeting (https://www.royalgreenwich.gov.uk/schools-and-education/services-schools/school-governors-support); Newham up to £200 per meeting paid to clerks. Three priced sellers for clerking; a priced *HR-panel specialist* clerk: not found.
- **Scarcity:** S7. Supply is short because clerking is low-paid, part-time, and evening-based, which suits this user exactly.
- **Timing:** steady; no dated trigger found.
- **Comparable revenue:** not found; per-meeting fees cap a solo at modest income (my arithmetic: 100 meetings x £250 = £25k, OPINION).
- **First sale:** possible within weeks through a council clerking service or trust advert; contracting through councils is slow.
- **User assets:** employment law, People leadership, evening availability. Belfast: NI schools are run by the Education Authority with its own structures (not checked).
- **Conflicts:** low with day job and Nudge.
- **Evidence against:** councils and Juniper already sell clerking; many clerks are employed, not freelance; England-focused; prize small; panels for dismissals may be daytime.

## L6. Fixed-fee employee DSAR handling for small employers (desk work, evenings)

**One line:** take an employee's subject access request off a 20-250-person employer: search plan, review, redaction, response letter, within the one-month limit.

- **Who pays:** small employers facing a DSAR from a departing or aggrieved employee.
- **Proof of pay:** Osborne Clarke quotes £500-2,000 for receipt and initial analysis, outsourced paralegals £700/day, own team £1,200/day (flyer dated 23 Apr 2026 in URL: https://www.osborneclarke.com/system/files/documents/26/04/23/DSAR-Management-Flyer%28160754552.4%29-%281%29.pdf); Ashtons Legal fixed-fee retainer (https://ashtonslegal.co.uk/your-business/employment-law-for-employers/data-subject-access-request-dsar); Prettys and Squire Patton Boggs (DSAResolution) also sell it. 3+ sellers, mostly law firms, some priced.
- **Scarcity:** weak. Evidence of thin supply for *small* employers: law-firm pricing implies a gap between DIY and £1,000s (possible 3x price gap, buyers in between not evidenced). No waiting lists found.
- **Timing:** Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 changed DSAR rules (reasonable and proportionate searches); commencement date not verified in this round.
- **Comparable revenue:** not found.
- **First sale:** plausible via HR consultants who get these requests from clients; untested.
- **User assets:** employment law (DSARs are usually pre-litigation), build skill (Claude Code redaction pipeline, run locally).
- **Conflicts:** low; but handling other employers' personal data at home needs ICO registration (a fee, not a licence) and security.
- **Evidence against:** eDiscovery/redaction tools exist; Microsoft 365 Purview does searches; risk of handling litigation-sensitive data solo; law firms bundle it with advice.

## L7. Independent appeal or grievance chair for owner-managed micro-firms (no one senior left to hear it)

**One line:** a fixed-fee, video-based independent chair for appeals where the owner is the only senior person and Acas guidance points to "an external HR consultant".

- **Who pays:** micro and small employers (under 20 staff), GB first.
- **Proof of pay:** WorkNest sells appeals "handled by a separate HR consultant" (https://worknest.com/employment-law-hr/hr-consultancy/investigations-hearings-and-appeals/); Frettens describes the independent chair role (https://www.frettens.co.uk/site/blog/employment-blog/what-is-the-role-of-an-independent-chair); TCM Group sells disciplinary hearing and appeal support. Prices: not found.
- **Scarcity:** guidance repeatedly tells small firms to use "an external HR consultant" when no impartial manager exists (Sprintlaw, TUC: https://www.tuc.org.uk/guidance/i-want-my-grievance-heard-independent-manager-can-be-arranged). That is a structural need; evidence of buyers unable to find one: not found.
- **Timing:** possible lift from the 6-month qualifying period from 1 Jan 2027 (from earlier rounds; not re-verified).
- **Comparable revenue:** not found.
- **First sale:** plausible through HR consultancies or accountants; untested.
- **User assets:** employment law, seniority.
- **Conflicts:** day-job outside-work clause; weekday hearings.
- **Evidence against:** in NI the LRA offers free independent appeal panels (https://www.lra.org.uk/node/799); in GB, Peninsula/WorkNest/Citation include this for retainer clients; overlaps with L1 and with generator M.
