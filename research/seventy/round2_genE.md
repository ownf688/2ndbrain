## The shared fact base (read once, applies to most ideas)

- FACT: EU flat €3 customs duty per item type (per declaration line) on parcels of €150 or less from outside the EU, live since 1 Jul 2026, scheduled to end Jul 2028 ([Revenue.ie press release](https://www.revenue.ie/en/corporate/press-office/press-releases/2026/pr-062526-customs-rules.aspx); [ecommercefulfilment.com](https://www.ecommercefulfilment.com/resources/news/duty-free-shipments-into-the-eu-will-end-1-july-2026/); O34 check file). Duty is generally not refunded on returns ([CCPC](https://ccpc.ie/customs)).
- FACT: a further €2 EU handling fee per declaration line is expected from 1 Nov 2026, on parcels of any value, still going through formal approval (delegated act) ([RSM](https://www.rsm.global/belgium/en/insights/eu-introduces-new-charges-low-value-imports); [pasqualepillitteri.it](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/18538/eu-handling-fee-2-euro-non-eu-parcels); [Latvia VID](https://www.vid.gov.lv/en/changes-1-july-and-1-november-2026)). So Christmas 2026 is the first Christmas where a GB parcel to an Irish consumer can carry €5 per item type plus VAT.
- FACT: goods shipped from NI to ROI/EU consumers do not face this duty; NI sellers already advertise "no extra duties to Ireland" ([NI Candle Supplies](https://www.nicandlesupplies.co.uk/blogs/news-and-updates/shipping-to-you-post-brexit); [Kitesurf Warehouse](https://kitesurfwarehouse.com/pages/shipping-delivery-information); [Meteor Space, 24 Jul 2026](https://www.meteorspace.com/2026/07/24/one-border-two-markets-how-northern-ireland-beats-the-eus-new-tariffs/)).
- CAUTION (REASONABLE GUESS, needs a customs check): GB stock moved into NI and then on to the EU is "at risk" and should pay EU duty when it enters NI, unless it qualifies for zero tariff under UK preferential origin. So "store in NI, ship duty-free" works cleanly for UK-origin goods with origin proof, much less so for goods made in China or Bangladesh. Most small clothing labels source abroad. Several 3PL marketing pages gloss over this.
- FACT: Irish consumer bodies are pushing shoppers toward .ie websites ([weare.ie](https://weare.ie/new-customs-rules-make-checking-for-a-ie-website-even-more-important/)). A Belfast seller is not ".ie", so the free public message works against NI sellers unless they say "ships from NI, no customs" loudly.
- Already killed and not repeated here: O34 (€3 landed-cost checker), O37 (Windsor Framework compliance helper), O31 (white-label resale), O33 (TikTok Shop ops), A11 (merch). B13/B21/B22/B23 (own label TikTok, pre-orders, NI/IE wholesale via Faire, own resale) already survived stage 2; ideas below must differ from them.

## E1. "EU door" fulfilment set-up for GB small brands via an NI 3PL partner

- Proof of pay: 3PLs sell the NI route directly. &Open runs an NI warehouse for duty-free EU gift delivery ([&Open help](https://help.andopen.co/en/articles/15648510-july-2026-eu-customs-changes-what-this-means-for-your-open-gift-shipments)); SJ Henderson Fulfilment pitched it at Newry Chamber, Jun 2026 ([PDF](https://newrychamber.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/PPT-Stephen-Henderson-SJ-Henderson-Fulfilment.pdf)); ShipBob lists Belfast ([ShipBob](https://www.shipbob.com/uk/?p=2174)). Pick-pack about $2/order, EU postage £8-12 per Practical Ecommerce ([link](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/northern-ireland-key-to-e-u-u-k-fulfillment)). Price of a standalone set-up service: not found.
- Rivals and gap: the 3PLs themselves (above), Meteor Space, ecommercefulfilment.com, plus free TSS and InterTradeIreland advice (O37). Gap: none clearly shown; 3PLs do onboarding free to win the contract.
- Timing: €3 live since Jul 2026; €2 fee from 1 Nov 2026 (expected). Window ends Jul 2028 when the flat duty is replaced.
- Comparable revenue: not found.
- First sale in 2 weeks/£500: possible as a consult, but the buyer's real decision (moving stock) takes months.
- Assets: Belfast location only.
- Evidence against: the origin problem (CAUTION above) kills it for most imported-stock brands; free government advice; the 3PL is the natural seller; overlaps O37's kill reason (free TSS). Expect check 3 and 4 fails.

## Overall view (OPINION)
- The NI dual-market edge is real and new (Jul 2026), but the people best placed to sell it already do: NI 3PLs, &Open, and NI retailers who simply say "no customs" on their own sites. As in round 1, the seam is under-served mainly because it is small.
- The "store in NI, ship duty-free" story only fully holds for UK-origin goods. Any idea that relies on GB brands moving imported stock to NI should get a customs check before scoring.
- Strongest on fit and speed: E2 (own label, ROI-facing, Christmas 2026) and E5 (duty-free EU staff gifts sold to HR peers), but E2 may be read as a variant of B13/B21, and E5 has a direct rival (&Open) with the same edge.
- E3 is the most novel (NI makers to ROI, no customs, Christmas), with no direct rival found, but proof of pay and comparable revenue are "not found".
- E1, E4, E7, E8 look likely to die on checks 3/4 (free government help, free platform features, or crowded RP services). E6 has a weekday problem.
- No idea here touches payments or remittance (NALA) or interview feedback (Nudge).
