## 3. Failures, shutdowns and platform risk

1. **Slack rate-limit change, 29 May 2025 (FACT, Slack changelog).** For commercially distributed apps *not* approved for the Slack Marketplace, `conversations.history` and `conversations.replies` drop to 1 request per minute and 15 objects per request. Applies now to new installs, and to existing installs from 3 March 2026. Marketplace-approved apps and internal apps are exempt. [Slack docs](https://docs.slack.dev/changelog/2025/05/29/rate-limit-changes-for-non-marketplace-apps), [FAQ](https://api.slack.com/changelog/2025-05-terms-rate-limit-update-and-faq). REASONABLE GUESS: a DM bot that receives replies via the Events API may not need these calls, but anything that reads thread or channel history (for example probe threading across interviewers if it reads past messages) will. Not being in the Marketplace is now a technical handicap, not just a discovery one.
2. **Slack legacy deprecations (FACT).** Legacy custom bots ended 31 March 2025, classic apps 31 March 2026. [Slack changelog](https://docs.slack.dev/changelog/2024-09-legacy-custom-bots-classic-apps-deprecation/). Any app built on old patterns had to be rebuilt.
3. **Slido turned off its Slack app (CLAIMED, vendor post)** two years after launch, saying it needed to rebuild "from the ground up". Date not confirmed. [Slido community](https://community.slido.com/welcome-1/index4.html).
4. **Standuply (CLAIMED, Latka):** bootstrapped Slack standup bot, revenue reportedly fell from $450K to $154K in a year. Crowded Slack category with Geekbot and free alternatives. [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/standuply).
5. **Google Hire (FACT, older, 2019):** Google shut its own ATS within about two years. Even a giant could not make a new ATS stick against incumbents. [NJBIA](https://njbia.org/?p=36741) (seen in search result).
6. **TalentPad (India) and Reworking (one-way video interviews):** TalentPad closed after about 18 months, founders said the market "was not large enough"; Reworking listed as out of business (CLAIMED). [People Matters](https://www.peoplematters.in/amp/news/online-recruitment-solutions/online-recruitment-company-talentpad-shut-shop-11860), [Pitchbook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/340808-23).
7. **ATS adds the feature (from earlier pass, 2026-10-03, not re-checked):** Ashby, Greenhouse, Lever, GoodTime and Spark Hire Recruit already send Slack feedback reminders. No named startup post-mortem blaming this was found.

No 2023-2026 post-mortem from a small interview-feedback tool was found. That is a gap, not evidence of safety.

## 5. "Built it at the day job, spun it out"

- **Slack** (from playbooks.md): internal chat at Tiny Speck's game studio. Worked because the whole company used it daily and the game failed, freeing the team.
- **Basecamp** (playbooks.md): 37signals' internal project tool for client work. Worked because they had an audience (blog) to sell to.
- **Gymdesk, GoProposal** (playbooks.md, P6): founders built what their own trade lacked and sold to peers.
- **Recruitee** (above): internal hiring tool for the founders' startup, then other startups asked for it. The closest analogue to Nudge, and it grew into a full ATS, not a plugin.
- **Teamtailor** (above): not an internal tool, but founders came from the same company and sold to the employer-branding buyer they understood.

**What made them work (OPINION):** (1) the founder or team went full-time once demand showed; none of the scaled ones stayed a side project; (2) the tool grew from a feature into the system of record (Recruitee became an ATS, Slack became the product); (3) a ready audience or network bought first. Nudge has the insider insight (P6) and some network, but is part-time and is deliberately staying a feature next to the ATS.

## OPINION

**Which playbook:** Nudge runs **P6, vertical tool for a profession you live in** (Head of People builds what hiring lacked, sells to peers), riding **P3, marketplace distribution** (Slack App Directory), with a little **P2** (Claude-era AI structuring). P6 in playbooks.md says 3 to 5 years to the bar; P3 says slow and low-revenue for apps.

**Likely path:**
- **Base case (most likely):** under $1k MRR within 12 months, like ~70-97% of micro-SaaS. The TrustMRR median is $169/month.
- **Good case:** $10k MRR (100-200 workspaces) in 2-4 years if the Marketplace listing lands and churn stays near 3%. That is the HeyTaco/Standuply band: a nice side income of GBP 100-350k a year at best.
- **$1m ARR:** I found no bootstrapped, part-time, solo Slack app that got there. Geekbot (CLAIMED $1m by 2020) had a team of 17+. At GBP 39-79 per workspace, $1m needs 800-1,700 paying teams after churn. The realistic $1m route is a sale to an ATS or interview-intelligence player (the Halp/Troops pattern), and both of those had raised venture money first. Probability of $1m ARR within 5 years as a part-time solo project: low, single digits in percent (OPINION).

**Three biggest lessons from failures:**
1. **Slack owns the rails.** The May 2025 rate limits penalise unlisted apps, and classic apps were switched off in March 2026. Getting Marketplace-approved is now survival, not marketing; the 5-10 active-workspace bar means the first 10 customers gate both revenue and the platform.
2. **The incumbent ships your feature.** Greenhouse, Ashby and Lever already do Slack reminders; Zoom bought BrightHire to own interview intelligence. The "chaser" part of Nudge is already commodity; only the 60-second free-text capture and probe threading are not, and those can be copied by an ATS in a quarter.
3. **Slack-app categories plateau and crowd.** Standuply shrank from $450K to $154K (CLAIMED) next to Geekbot; HeyTaco sits under $500K. Cheap per-workspace pricing caps revenue. Price per hiring team or per interview, and sell to companies already paying for an ATS, or the ceiling arrives early.
