# Bets for a solo builder in Belfast: what the millionaires did, and what to try

Research date: 2026-10-03. Research only, nothing was built. Stage 2 working files are in `research/millionaires/`: `cases/` (9 files), `base_rates.md`, `playbooks.md`, `matches.md`, `bets_candidates.md`, `checks/B1.md` to `checks/B32.md`, `critic.md`, `SOURCES.md`. Stage 1 is in `research/REPORT.md`.

> **How far to trust the numbers.** Of 84 "millionaire" cases found, only **10 have verified figures** (payment data, filed accounts or a confirmed sale price). The other 74 are **claimed** (interviews, founder posts, vendor case studies). This environment blocks most sites, so nearly every figure was seen in a search result, not on the original page. Every money figure below says VERIFIED or CLAIMED. Income estimates are arithmetic on cited rates, labelled REASONABLE GUESS, and are **sales, not profit**, unless stated.

## 1. One-page summary

**What I did.** Nine research agents collected 84 cases of solo founders and teams of 5 or fewer who reached £1m+ (or $1m+) in yearly sales, profit or sale price, mostly from 2021 to 2026. A tenth collected the base rates: what usually happens. I pulled out the repeatable moves, matched them to the six strong patterns from stage 1, generated 32 bets in two rounds, and ran every bet through the same kill checks as stage 1 plus a check that it fits your limits. A critic then attacked the survivors.

**What the millionaires really looked like.**
- **Most had a head start.** Where the background is known (49 cases), 20 already had an audience, 14 had a rare skill and 7 built from their own day job. Only 1 had nothing.
- **Most rode a named wave.** 68 of 84 rode one: AI models (about 15), remote work (about 12) or social commerce (about 7). Being early mattered more than being clever. The AI-tools file found 9 of 12 AI winners launched within about 6 months of a new model ability, and 7 of those 12 products are now free features elsewhere.
- **Most started cheap.** 8 of the 10 that state their starting money began with about £5,000 or less.
- **It took time.** Median time to £1m was about 1.8 years where stated (27 cases). Non-AI software took about 3 years and remote service businesses 4 to 5.
- **The typical outcome is almost nothing.** The median Stripe-verified indie product earns **$169 a month** (VERIFIED, TrustMRR sample of 5,079). Only about 38% of new UK registered businesses survive five years (VERIFIED, ONS). £1m sits at or beyond the top 1% in every category with data.

**What survived for you.** 32 bets went in. **3 survived the kill checks and the critic**, and all 3 sell something only you own:
1. **B13: sell your own clothing label live on TikTok Shop**, with pre-orders and seconds as add-ons.
2. **B20: sell sponsor slots in your HR-leaders newsletter** to HR-tech vendors, with sponsored reader surveys as an add-on.
3. **B22: wholesale your label to independent shops in NI and Ireland** through Faire and Ankorstore.

The other 29 died, mostly because a funded body or an incumbent gives the same thing away free (Acas, Skills for Care, Breathe, Octopus, Notion), because cheap rivals already sell it, or because free AI does it. **That is fewer than the 10 you asked for.** The list below adds 6 clearly labelled **near-misses** to reach 9. I left slot 10 empty because nothing else came close enough.

**My honest read (OPINION).** None of the 9 shows a path to £1m within your limits. Each is a test that turns an asset you already have into money and tells you which asset is worth building. The pattern that best fits both you and the millionaire evidence is **audience first, then product** (20 cases, the most common single move). Your HR-leaders newsletter is that audience. So the real long-term bet is to **grow the list**, and B20 is the cheapest way to price it. The biggest unknown is your list size, which this research could not find. Find it out first: it decides whether five of the nine cards are worth running.

## 3. The cards

All dates assume the test starts in the week of **12 October 2026**. "Hours" are your evening and weekend hours. Kill date = the day you stop if the pass number has not been reached.

### Card 1. B13: Sell your own label live on TikTok Shop (survivor)
- **The bet:** run evening TikTok Shop lives selling your existing clothing stock, and use pre-orders to fund new styles and Vinted Pro to clear seconds.
- **Pattern and case:** pattern P7 (second-hand and social shopping grow). Copies **Made by Mitchell**, a UK brand that sold $1m on TikTok Shop in one day through a 12-hour live (CLAIMED, [ChannelX, Jun 2024](https://channelx.world/2024/06/uks-made-by-mitchell-sells-1-million-in-24-hours-on-tiktok-shop)), at a much smaller scale. Also **Comfrt** (TikTok creator affiliates, CLAIMED; see `cases/shops-brands.md`).
- **Who pays, how much, proof:** shoppers, at your label's current prices (your prices were not provided). Proof the channel pays: 300,000+ UK small businesses sell on TikTok Shop and live sales are up 55% a year (CLAIMED by the platform, [Retail Gazette, Jul 2026](https://www.retailgazette.co.uk/blog/2026/07/tiktok-shop-hits-300000-uk-small-business-sellers-as-live-sales-surge/)). Fees: 9% commission, possibly 4% for a new shop's first 60 days ([EcomCalcTools](https://ecomcalctools.com/blog/fees-tiktok/tiktok-shop-uk/)).
- **First 10 customers, step by step:**
  1. Open a TikTok Shop seller account and list 5+ products, which unlocks the new-seller commission rate.
  2. Post 6-10 short videos of the products over the first week. Each one tells viewers the time of the next live.
  3. Message your existing label followers and email list with the live times: Tuesday and Thursday at 8pm, and Sunday afternoon.
  4. Invite 3-5 small UK fashion creators through TikTok's free Affiliate Center, offering free samples and commission.
  5. Run the lives with a "live-only" price on one item. Ask every buyer to tag a friend.
  6. List 10-20 seconds and samples on Vinted Pro (0% seller fee, [Vinted Pro](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/vinted-pro-launches-in-uk/)) under the brand name.
- **Two-week test with real money:** spend up to **£250** (creator samples at cost, about £100 of TikTok promotion for 2 lives, packaging). Run **4 lives in 14 days**. **Pass: £500+ in TikTok Shop sales in 14 days** (enough to cover the spend twice) **and at least one live with 50+ peak viewers. Fail: under £150 in sales.** Anything between means one more fortnight, then decide. Pre-order step, only after a pass: open a pre-order on one new style with a **30-unit threshold in 14 days. Fail = refund everyone.**
- **Cost:** £250 to £500. About 20 hours over 2 weeks (6 setup, 8 live, 6 packing and posting).
- **What you'd need to learn:** TikTok Shop seller tools and live-selling basics, about one weekend. The unit-cost sum per item (cost, 9% fee, returns), one evening.
- **Kill date:** **31 December 2026.** Stop if total TikTok Shop sales across Black Friday and December are under **£1,000**.
- **12-month income (sales, not profit; REASONABLE GUESS from `millionaires/critic.md`):**
  - **Low, about £0 to £1,000:** lives stay under 100 viewers, at the claimed "hobby tier" of about $120 a stream (CLAIMED).
  - **Middle, about $13,800:** the claimed US median active TikTok Shop seller, $1,150 a month (CLAIMED, [Ringly](https://www.ringly.io/blog/tiktok-shop-statistics-2026)).
  - **High, about $80,000:** two lives a week at the claimed small-tier $800 a stream (CLAIMED, [Dashboardly](https://www.dashboardly.io/statistics/tiktok-shop-live-shopping-statistics)).
  - Then take off 9% commission and fashion returns, claimed at 18-25%. Profit was not found, because your unit costs are unknown.
- **Biggest risk:** a new account gets almost no live viewers. Second: going live in public every week sits awkwardly with a senior people role, so check your outside-work clause.

### Card 2. B20: Sell sponsor slots in your HR-leaders newsletter (survivor)
- **The bet:** sell a clearly labelled sponsor slot in each issue to HR-tech vendors, priced per 1,000 subscribers, and later offer one sponsored reader survey a year.
- **Pattern and case:** pattern P3 (value moves to trust and distribution). Copies **TLDR** (free newsletter for one profession, paid for by vendors; $5m a year with 0 employees at one stage, CLAIMED, [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/tldr-newsletter-breakdown)). Also **The Neuron** (2 founders; claimed $825k revenue and $500k profit in 18 months, then sold to TechnologyAdvice for "seven figures" in January 2025, CLAIMED, [beehiiv case study](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/case-study-the-neuron)). The closest UK model is Hung Lee's **Recruiting Brainfood**, which says 30,000+ recruiters and HR people read it. Its revenue was not found.
- **Who pays, how much, proof:** HR-tech vendors (HR systems, payroll, benefits, learning). Rates found:
  - Paved's Q3 2026 benchmark: HR newsletters earn **$18.47 per 1,000 subscribers** per send, but that is an average over lists averaging 24,439 subscribers ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/paved-finds-hr-newsletter-ad-rates-run-15-times-higher-than-lifestyle-lists/)).
  - Working Families (12,000+ subscribers): **£250 per insert** ([rate card](https://workingfamilies.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Working-Families-Sponsorship-Rate-Card.pdf)).
  - HRAddict: **$350 for 3 placements** ([Passionfroot](https://www.passionfroot.me/hraddict)).
  - Large B2B publishers charge **$11,750-19,500 per sponsored survey** ([Demand Gen Report](https://mediakit.demandgenreport.com/?p=1742)). No small-list survey price was found.
  - All seen 2026-10-03.
- **First 10 customers, step by step:**
  1. **Before anything else:** read your contract's outside-interests clause. Declare the newsletter and the sponsor model to your employer in writing and get written consent.
  2. Write an exclusion list: current suppliers, anyone in an open tender, any vendor you evaluated in the last 12 months, and every interview-feedback or interview-intelligence vendor (Nudge rivals).
  3. Build a one-page media kit: subscriber count, open rate, reader job titles, price per send. Put it on Passionfroot (free) and apply to the beehiiv ad network or Paved.
  4. Make a list of 20 HR-tech vendors that sell into UK/IE mid-size firms and are not on the exclusion list. Find their marketing leads on LinkedIn.
  5. Email or DM 15 of them a "founding sponsor" offer, with a 20% discount on the first 2 slots and a free performance report afterwards.
  6. Add a footer line: "Sponsored slots are labelled. The author co-founded Nudge."
- **Two-week test with real money:** your spend is near zero (optionally up to £80 for one month of LinkedIn Sales Navigator). The real money is the sponsor's. **Pass: 2 sponsor slots paid for (money received) within 14 days of the first pitch, at no less than the Paved rate ($18.47 per 1,000 subscribers). Fail: 0 paid slots.** One paid slot means pitch 15 more and decide by the kill date.
- **Cost:** £0 to £80. About 10 hours (consent request 1, media kit 3, vendor list 2, outreach 4).
- **What you'd need to learn:** newsletter ad pricing and media kits, about 2 evenings. ASA "ad" labelling rules, 1 evening.
- **Kill date:** **30 November 2026** if fewer than 2 paid slots are booked. Stop at once if your employer refuses consent.
- **12-month income (REASONABLE GUESS from `critic.md`; weekly send; low = 12 sold sends, middle = 26):**

| List size | Low | Middle | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | about $220 | about $480 | about $1,800 (CLAIMED beehiiv range, top end × 12) |
| 5,000 | about $1,100 | about $2,400 | about $9,600 (same source) |
| 10,000 | about $2,200 | about $4,800 | about £13,000 (Working Families insert rate × 52) |

- **Biggest risk:** the conflict of interest. You are a buyer of HR tech in your day job, and taking vendor money can look like an inducement, which could be a Bribery Act issue (REASONABLE GUESS, not legal advice). Excluding conflicted vendors and Nudge rivals may also shrink the pool of sponsors a lot. The second risk is list size: below a few thousand, vendors may not bother.

### Card 3. B22: Wholesale your label to independent shops in NI and Ireland (survivor)
- **The bet:** list the label on Faire and Ankorstore, and sell into NI and Irish boutiques and gift shops through your own commission-free Faire Direct link.
- **Pattern and case:** pattern P7. Copies the **70+ UK brands that have made over $1M wholesale on Faire** (CLAIMED by Faire, [Business Wire, Jun 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260611115823/en/)). No named solo case was found.
- **Who pays, how much, proof:**
  - Independent retailers pay wholesale prices, usually about half of retail (REASONABLE GUESS, not sourced).
  - Faire takes 15% on repeat orders and up to 25% on a first order from a new retailer, and 0% through your Faire Direct link ([Craftybase](https://craftybase.com/blog/how-much-does-faire-charge)).
  - Ankorstore has a €100 minimum order. It may have moved to 0% on reorders in January 2026 (CLAIMED, re-check).
  - Proof shops pay: Faire has 100,000+ retailers, and the UK is its largest market outside North America ([InsightDIY](https://www.insightdiy.co.uk/news/faire-marks-five-years-in-the-uk-as-network-reaches-15000-locations/16573.htm)).
- **First 10 customers, step by step:**
  1. Do the unit-cost sum first. If wholesale price minus 25% doesn't beat your cost per unit, stop here.
  2. List on Faire and Ankorstore (both free to list).
  3. Build a list of 30 NI and Irish independent clothing and gift shops from Instagram and Google Maps.
  4. DM and email each one a short lookbook and your Faire Direct link. Goods move from NI to Ireland without customs checks under the Windsor Framework (REASONABLE GUESS, not checked in this stage).
  5. Post a sample to the 10 that reply.
  6. Follow up after 7 days with a first-order discount.
- **Two-week test with real money:** spend up to **£300** (sample stock at cost and postage to 10 shops). **Pass: 2 wholesale orders of at least €100 each within 14 days of samples landing. Fail: 0 orders.**
- **Cost:** £300 to £500. About 12 hours.
- **What you'd need to learn:** wholesale pricing and line sheets, about 1 weekend.
- **Kill date:** **31 January 2027.** Stop if there are fewer than 3 stockists and no reorder.
- **12-month income (wholesale sales before commission; REASONABLE GUESS from `critic.md`):**
  - **Low, about £300 to £600:** 3 shops place one €100-200 order each.
  - **Middle, about $6,000:** 15 shops × $200 average order (CLAIMED average, North America) × 2 orders.
  - **High, about $60,000:** an agency's claim of $5K a month for new brands (CLAIMED, treat it as a ceiling).
- **Biggest risk:** margin. Small production runs (about 50 units per style) at half retail, minus up to 25%, may lose money. Stock has to be bought up front unless pre-orders fund it.

### Which 3 to run first, and in what order (OPINION)
1. **This week: find out your newsletter's subscriber count and open rate, and send the B20 consent request to your employer.** This costs nothing. The answer decides whether B20, B1, B17 and B31 are worth running at all.
2. **First test: B13 (TikTok Shop live), starting by mid-October.** It needs nobody's permission, uses stock you already have, and the peak season (Black Friday was TikTok Shop UK's biggest sales day in 2025, [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/retail/tiktok-shop-records-its-highest-sales-day-in-the-uk-for-black-friday/2025120385176)) is 6 weeks away. Timing is the only edge it has.
3. **Second test: B20 (sponsor slots), as soon as consent is in writing.** It uses your rarest asset and costs nothing. A pass also prices the list for everything else.
4. **Third test: B5 (done-for-you client newsletter).** It is the cheapest decisive test among the expertise bets, and if it works it adds a second income from the same expertise. Run it only if B20's consent is still pending or B20 has failed, so you never run more than two tests at once.

### Which bets share customers or channels
- **Label cluster (shoppers and shops):** B13 → its pre-orders and seconds → B22. Lives build the followers that make a pre-order hit its minimum. Pre-orders fund the stock that B22 needs. Shops see sell-through on TikTok as proof.
- **Newsletter cluster (HR leads and HR-tech vendors):** B20 → B28 survey → B17 job slots → B31 webinars. These are the same vendors and the same media kit, and each sale makes the next pitch easier. B1 (paid tier) shares the same readers.
- **Expertise cluster (GB+NI employment law):** B1 → B5 → B3. B1 proves readers value your take, B5 resells the same writing to consultancies, and B3 turns it into a live format.
- **No overlap between clusters except your time.** Never run two clusters' tests in the same fortnight.

### If you could only pick one: B20, but only with written employer consent (OPINION)
- **Why:** the most common millionaire move in the evidence is an audience first (20 of the 49 cases where the head start is known). The professional-newsletter route (TLDR, The Neuron, Pragmatic Engineer) is the only one where your existing asset, a list of HR leaders, is the thing buyers pay for.
- **What it gives you:** B20 is the cheapest way to learn what that list is worth. It also opens the long-term route: a niche professional newsletter that a vendor eventually buys, as TechnologyAdvice bought The Neuron.
- **What it won't do:** it will not make much money in year one.
- **If your employer says no:** back **B13** instead. It is the only bet with a seasonal timing edge right now and no conflict.

## 5. What the millionaires had in common, in 10 lines

1. Most had a head start: 20 of 49 known cases had an audience before they launched. Only 1 had nothing (`playbooks.md`).
2. 68 of 84 rode a named wave: AI models, remote work or social commerce. Timing did more work than talent.
3. Being early to a new ability paid off fast, but the window closed in 6 to 18 months. 7 of 12 AI winners' features are now free elsewhere.
4. They picked one profession or one trade and stayed narrow (GoProposal, Gymdesk, Bookkeeping for Painters).
5. Most started cheap: 8 of 10 stated starts were £5,000 or less.
6. It still took time: about 1.8 years median to £1m, and 3 to 5 years outside AI.
7. Where we know how they found customers, it was through an audience, referrals or a platform's algorithm. Paid ads (1 case) and cold outreach (0 cases) almost never appear.
8. Several crossed the bar by **selling the business**, not by yearly sales (8 cases). Newsletters especially sold to vendors who wanted the audience.
9. Only 10 of 84 have verified figures, and at least 9 sell a course about their own success. Treat the stories as advertising.
10. The people who ran the same playbooks and failed (HouseFresh, All About Berlin, PDF-chat wrappers) mostly depended on one channel they didn't own (`base_rates.md`).
