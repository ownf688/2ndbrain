## Part B: Near-misses

### NM1: Done-for-you monthly client newsletter for UK (and NI) employment-law firms and HR consultancies

**Rivals found (FACT unless marked):**
- **The Content Club** (Consortium): from £300/month for 4 solicitor-reviewed articles a month, employment law included, aimed at small regional law firms ([coastcommunications](https://coastcommunications.co.uk/marketing-agency-launches-the-content-club-for-small-law-regional-firms/), [Today's Conveyancer](https://todaysconveyancer.co.uk/legal-marketing-company-launches-content-service-law-firms/)).
- **Emplaw**: a white-label employment-law email newsletter for firms (written, customised, designed, plus a sending tool) and a branded sub-site ([infolaw](https://infolaw.co.uk/newsletter/?p=106), [infolaw](https://www.infolaw.co.uk/newsletter/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/266)). Price not found. The source pages may be old.
- **HRi (HR Independents)**: membership for independent HR consultants. Core £49+VAT/month; Premium £65+VAT/month includes "white label content" and a template library ([hrindependents.co.uk](https://hrindependents.co.uk/hri-membership-plans/)). This is the most direct cheap rival for the HR-consultancy half.
- **WorkNest, NORI HR, Qdos**: white-label HR and employment-law partnerships sold under the consultant's own brand ([WorkNest](https://worknest.com/partnerships/white-label)). Prices not found.
- **Legal Island** (Belfast): NI Employment Law Hub at £295+VAT/year for 1 user up to £895+VAT/year for 10 users, with 1,000+ NI subscribers ([legal-island.com/subscribe](https://legal-island.com/subscribe), [legal-island hub](https://legal-island.com/employment-law-hub)). It is a reader subscription, not a done-for-you newsletter, but it is the obvious NI incumbent if it chose to white-label.
- Free NI updates: TLT quarterly, 3PB / Emma McIlveen monthly, Gateley and Pinsent Masons webinars ([TLT](https://tlt.com/insights-and-events/publications/ni-quarterly-employment-update), [3PB](https://www.3pb.co.uk/?p=39329)).
- From O13: Caramail from $299/month in the Clio app directory, and LandmarkPD Fee Builder for accountants at £55/month.
- Franchise networks write their own: The HR Dept sends a central "People Matter" newsletter for its franchisees ([hrdept.co.uk](https://www.hrdept.co.uk/Highbury%20&%20islington/knowledge-hub)).

**How weak is the evidence?** FACT: people pay for content subscriptions in this area (Content Club, HRi Premium, Legal Island). Not found: anyone paying specifically for a done-for-you, sent, branded monthly newsletter for an HR consultancy. REASONABLE GUESS: demand exists but at low prices, because HRi bundles white-label content for £65/month.

**3 most likely reasons it fails (OPINION):**
1. HR consultancies are often one person on thin margins. They already get "good enough" white-label content from HRi (£65) or the free law-firm updates, and they can rewrite those with a free AI.
2. Law firms will not send legal updates written by a non-solicitor without their own review. That cuts the time saved and pushes them towards Content Club-style solicitor-reviewed content.
3. A monthly deadline on top of a full-time job, the user's own HR newsletter and a clothing label: delivery slips, and churn follows.

**Verdict: NEAR-MISS (weak).** The only possible edge is GB plus NI law in one issue, written by a practitioner and fully sent for the client. FACT: NI employment law differs from GB ([CIPD factsheet](https://cipd.org/uk/knowledge/factsheets/northern-ireland-differences-factsheet)). The GB content services found do not mention NI. No Nudge overlap.
- **Who pays:** NI and GB independent HR consultancies (first) and small employment-law practices (second).
- **Price range (from rivals only):** £65/month (HRi Premium) to £300/month (Content Club). Caramail is about $299/month.
- **Biggest risk:** HRi already covers the low end for £65, and a free AI covers the rest. Prices are pinned near the bottom.
- **One-week test (real money):** a one-page offer with a Stripe link: "Founding client: your branded GB+NI employment-law newsletter, written and sent monthly, £149/month, first issue in November, full refund if you cancel before it goes out." Spend £150 on LinkedIn ads targeting UK/NI HR-consultancy owners. Also send it once to the user's HR-leaders newsletter list and to 30 direct messages to NI HR consultants. **Pass:** 3 or more paid pre-sales in 7 days. **Fail:** 0-1. Two means run a second week.
- **Scoring inputs:**
  - Proof people pay: medium for content subs, nil for this exact product.
  - How empty the field is: partly filled (HRi, Emplaw, Content Club, Legal Island). The NI-plus-GB done-for-you slot was not seen filled.
  - Timing: REASONABLE GUESS, a steady stream of GB Employment Rights Act changes in 2026-27 keeps updates in demand. No single dated deadline drives a purchase.
  - Size of prize: small. REASONABLE GUESS: tens of clients at £65-300, not hundreds.
  - Speed to first sale: fast (days), because the user has a list.
  - Fit: high on expertise and channel, low on time.

### NM2: AI hiring-tool due-diligence pack (vendor questionnaire, DPIA, candidate notice, bias checklist)

**Rivals and free substitutes found (FACT):**
- APSCo gives members the "Top 10 questions to ask AI system suppliers" checklist by Squire Patton Boggs for free. Squire Patton Boggs updated its recruitment due-diligence guide in July 2026 ([APSCo](https://au.apsco.org/resources/due-diligence-checklist-top-10-questions-to-ask-ai-system-suppliers-squire-patton-boggs-255928662213), [SPB AI hub](https://aihub.squirepattonboggs.com/2026/07/ai-tools-a-due-diligence-guide-for-the-recruitment-sector/)).
- The ICO publishes questions to ask before buying an AI tool. Its "Recruitment Rewired" report on automated recruitment came out on 31 Mar 2026 ([Kingsley Napley](https://www.kingsleynapley.co.uk/insights/blogs/public-law-blog/recruitment-rewired-what-employers-need-to-know-about-automated-recruitment), [technologyslegaledge](https://www.technologyslegaledge.com/2026/04/uk-ico-report-on-automated-decision-making-in-recruitment/)).
- Free downloadable checklists: TalentMatched UK AI compliance checklist (.docx), Hyring AI recruitment checklist ([talentmatched](https://www.talentmatched.com/compliancedocs/UK%20AI%20Compliance%20Checklist.docx), [hyring](https://hyring.com/free-hr-toolkit/checklists/ai-recruitment-checklist)).
- Paid: Sprintlaw (fixed-fee law firm) sells AI governance and AI use policies for recruitment agencies, typically £100-1,500 per document ([sprintlaw AI governance](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/data-privacy/ai-governance-policy/), [sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/ai-contract-review/), [AI use policies for agencies](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/ai-use-policies-for-uk-recruitment-agencies/)).
- Vendor side: Warden AI has no public price; vendors such as Sense and inploi publish their Warden verification so buyers see it free ([Sense](https://sensehq.com/blog/sense-earns-warden-verification-a-new-milestone-in-responsible-ai), [inploi](https://www.inploi.com/blog/fair-transparent-and-audited-how-inploi-is-raising-the-bar-for-ai-in-talent-acquisition)). XOR from $12,000 (from O19).

**Timing (FACT, from search results):** the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 automated decision-making changes applied from 5 Feb 2026. They require transparency, meaningful human review and a way to contest decisions ([comparethecloud](https://www.comparethecloud.net/articles/ai-tools-uk-recruitment-agencies-automated-decision-making-rules)). These dates are already past, and lawyers and trade bodies have already published free guidance on them.

**How weak is the evidence?** Very weak. No buyer was found paying for a due-diligence pack. The closest paid item is Sprintlaw's lawyer-drafted policy, which is a regulated-firm product.

**3 most likely reasons it fails (OPINION):**
1. The trade bodies (APSCo, REC partners) and the ICO give the core content away, and a free AI fills the gaps.
2. Buyers who want comfort want a law firm's name (Sprintlaw, SPB) or a vendor's Warden badge, not a template from a solo builder.
3. **Nudge conflict.** Nudge is itself a hiring-intelligence tool that AI-tool buyers would run this kind of due diligence on. Selling buyers the checklist used to judge your own product's category is a credibility and conflict problem.

**Verdict: DEAD.** Free inputs, free professional-body checklists, no paid demand found, and a direct conflict with Nudge.

## Bottom line (OPINION)
Every Phase 4 kill stands. NM1 is the only idea worth a real-money test, and it is weak: the price ceiling is set by HRi at £65/month and Content Club at £300/month, and the size of the prize is small. Treat the test as a way to find out whether there is a niche lifestyle income here, not a venture.
