# Pulse Intelligence

## What this is

Structured capture and analysis of NALA's quarterly "Feedback Loop" pulse surveys run via Windmill. These are the org's primary voice-of-employee channel and drive the public People team roadmap in Notion.

## Pulse architecture

6 department-level pulses, 12-week recurring cycle, anonymous responses. Questions: "What's working well at NALA?" / "What's not working well at NALA?"

## How to use this note

Each quarterly run gets:
1. A **run analysis** section below (dated, with response rates, themes, and verbatim signals)
2. **Cross-quarter theme tracking** in the Themes Tracker table (trending up/down/new/resolved)
3. **Roadmap actions** linking themes to People team deliverables (with DRI, deadline, status)

When a theme appears 2+ quarters running without action, flag it RED in the next brief.

### Eng & Prod (6 responses)

**Working well:** Technical grooming improving alignment. Good delivery pace. Refactoring time appreciated. Strong team spirit. Mission motivating. Mobile chapter technical excellence (linting, CI/CD).

**Not working (critical):**
- **Strategic direction (3/6):** "Can't prioritise... trying to do too much, to not a high enough standard." "Plans changing very quickly and very often." "Many bets but we can't stick with one long enough."
- **Talent attrition (1/6):** "Losing great people all the time, due to frustration and lack of direction."
- **Performative comms (1/6):** "Companies that have good morale don't need to tell employees that things are good... being told those things ends up demoralising people."

### Cross-Department Analysis

**Strongest negative signal: Strategic direction and prioritisation.** Three independent Eng & Prod respondents describe the same problem in different words: NALA changes direction too often, tries to do too much, and pushes people to meet "made up deadlines" on work that "doesn't end up delivering value." This is corroborated by the attrition signal ("losing great people... due to frustration and lack of direction") and the direction uncertainty flagged by a fifth respondent. This is not a People-fixable issue - it needs exec attention.

**Second signal: Product-Ops coordination gap.** Two Operations respondents independently flag the same issue: products and promotions launch without CS being briefed. This is fixable with a process: 48hr pre-launch briefing SLA between Product and CS.

**Third signal: Nairobi office perks regression.** Small but symbolic - removing fruit is noticed and interpreted as a step backward. Cheap to fix, high signal-to-cost ratio.

**Positive signals:** Team spirit and collaboration are strong across all departments. Hybrid work is very popular in Nairobi. Technical execution in Eng is healthy. AI adoption in Ops is generating optimism.

### What / So What / Now What / When

**What:** 15 responses across 4 departments (2 silent). Dominant theme: Eng & Prod sees a strategic direction crisis; Ops sees a product-CS coordination gap.

**So What:** The direction theme is the most dangerous signal in this dataset. If it persists, it correlates with the attrition risk already flagged. This is the "cost of doing nothing" - talented people leave because they don't see coherent strategy, and the remaining team grinds harder on shifting priorities. The Product-CS gap is lower risk but directly affects customer experience (Customers First value).
