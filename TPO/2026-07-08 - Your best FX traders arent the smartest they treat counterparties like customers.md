# Your best FX traders aren't the smartest - they're the ones who treat counterparties like customers

## Take

The highest-performing traders in emerging market FX corridors don't win on speed or pricing. They win because they build trust-based relationships with counterparties that competitors treat as arm's-length transaction endpoints. The delta between a good desk and a great one isn't the pricing engine - it's whether your counterparties pick up the phone when you need liquidity in an hour.

## Insight

Every B2B marketplace with a liquidity constraint has the same structural problem: execution is table stakes, but relationship depth determines who gets access to scarce supply. In FX trading for African corridors, this means the trader who treats a counterparty like a customer - understanding their business, staying in touch when there's no trade to do, growing their volumes from $1k to $15M through personal trust - will outperform the technically superior trader who views counterparties as numbers on a screen. The implication for hiring: stop screening for technical knowledge (everyone has Bloomberg on their CV) and start screening for relationship-building evidence. Ask candidates: "Tell me about a counterparty relationship you built from zero. What did the first 6 months look like?"

- The same candidate built an FX desk from scratch at a company where customers had disappeared - not by offering better rates, but by rebuilding banking infrastructure and KYC processes that made the business trustworthy again.

## First Draft

### Your Best FX Traders Aren't the Smartest - They're the Ones Who Treat Counterparties Like Customers

Every fintech CFO I know is hiring FX traders right now. The brief is always the same: "We need someone who knows African corridors, has Bloomberg experience, and can execute at pace."

They're hiring for the wrong thing.

**The traders who generate disproportionate value don't win on execution speed or pricing. They win because counterparties pick up the phone when they need liquidity in an hour.**

I sat in on a bar-raiser interview last week for a senior FX trading role. The candidate had two years of trading experience. Not twenty. Two. But he'd grown a single counterparty relationship from $1,000 to $15 million in volume. When he changed companies, customers followed him by name. His previous employer's customers called the new company specifically asking for him.

His thesis was disarmingly simple: "What distinguishes you from every other trader is having a personal relationship with customers."

This isn't soft. This is structural.

### The Counterparty-as-Customer Frame

Most FX desks treat counterparties as transaction endpoints. You put out a price quote on WhatsApp every morning to 20 firms. Some bite, some don't. You execute. You move on.

The problem is that this treats liquidity as a commodity. It isn't. In emerging market corridors - particularly East and West Africa - liquidity is scarce, episodic, and relationship-gated. When a large B2B client needs $1 million in Ugandan shillings in the next hour, the desk that has deep, trust-based counterparty relationships will source it. The desk that blasts WhatsApp quotes will not.

The CFO in this interview named the gap explicitly: "We haven't been treating FX counterparties as if they are also customers." This is a company with record B2B revenue - their best three months ever. The execution layer works. But the relationship layer is under-invested, and it's constraining growth.

### What This Means for Hiring

If the value creation happens in the relationship, not the execution, then your hiring criteria need to reflect that.

Stop screening for:
- Years of experience (this candidate had two)
- Platform knowledge (everyone lists Bloomberg and Refinitiv)
- Technical knowledge of options, futures, ALM

Start screening for:
- **Relationship evidence.** "Tell me about a counterparty relationship you built from zero. What did the first six months look like? How did volumes change?"
- **Customer follow.** "Has a customer ever followed you when you changed companies? Why?"
- **Infrastructure building.** The best traders don't just trade. They build the systems that make trading possible - banking relationships, KYC processes, settlement infrastructure. This candidate rebuilt an entire FX desk at a company where customers had disappeared. Not by offering better rates. By making the business trustworthy again.

### The Generalisation

This isn't just an FX insight. Every B2B marketplace with a supply constraint has the same dynamic:

**Recruitment:** The best recruiters don't have the biggest LinkedIn InMail budget. They have relationships with candidates who take their calls because they've been treated well for years - even when there's no live role.

**Enterprise sales:** The deals that close fastest aren't the ones with the best pricing. They're the ones where the buyer trusts the seller enough to skip a round of due diligence.

**VC deal flow:** The funds that see the best companies aren't the ones with the biggest brand. They're the ones where founders call them first because a portfolio company CEO vouched for how they behaved during a down round.

In every case, the pattern is identical: treat the supply side like customers, not like transaction endpoints. Invest in the relationship when there's no immediate payoff. The compounding effect is enormous.

### The Uncomfortable Implication

If you buy this frame, it changes how you evaluate tenure.

A trader with two years of experience who built a desk from scratch and has customers following them by name is more valuable than a trader with ten years at a large bank who executed on an established desk. The short tenure isn't a red flag - it's evidence of environments that demanded relationship building, not just execution.

This runs counter to most hiring heuristics in financial services, where tenure equals credibility. But in emerging market FX, where the market is being built rather than maintained, the builder's CV looks different from the operator's CV.

### What to Do

Pull your last 5 FX trader interviews. Count how many questions were about technical knowledge versus relationship evidence.

If the ratio is more than 2:1 in favour of technical, you're hiring executors when you need relationship builders.

Ask this in your next interview: "Walk me through the last counterparty relationship you built from nothing. How long did it take before they trusted you with a large trade? What did you do differently from other traders they work with?"

The answer will tell you more about their likely performance than any question about Bloomberg functionality ever will.
