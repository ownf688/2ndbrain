# The People Operator

Content seeds from Owen's daily work as Head of People at NALA. Each note is an atomic insight, framework, war story, or contrarian take -- shaped for turning into blog posts, LinkedIn pieces, or talk material.

## How it works

Notes land here automatically during normal brain operations:
- Meeting ingestion flags moments where Owen articulated a framework, gave pushback, or made a non-obvious call
- Decision logging captures any decision with a rationale that would resonate beyond NALA
- Pulse/performance analysis surfaces patterns worth writing about
- Hiring radar catches war stories and process innovations

## Taxonomy

**Types:**
- `insight` -- an observation or lesson learned
- `framework` -- a repeatable model others can apply
- `war-story` -- what actually happened (anonymised for publication)
- `contrarian-take` -- a position that challenges conventional People/HR wisdom
- `pattern` -- something you've seen 3+ times that deserves a name

**Themes:** hiring, performance, culture, remote-teams, people-ops, strategy, leadership, compensation, onboarding, org-design, feedback, data

**Heat:**
- `hot` -- strong conviction + evidence, write soon
- `warm` -- good seed, needs more evidence or shaping
- `cold` -- captured for completeness, may never publish

**Status:** seed > draft-ready > published

## Pipeline

Each seed contains a `## First Draft` section that gets populated when the seed moves to `draft-ready`. Drafts follow the structure defined in [[TPO Style Guide]] -- researched from 40+ professional blogs/newsletters including Lenny Rachitsky, Jessica Zwaan, Adam Grant, Wes Kao, DHH, First Round Review, and others.

**Draft structure:** Hook (2-3 sentences) > Setup (1 para) > Thesis (1 bold sentence) > Evidence sections (2-4, each Claim > Evidence > Implication) > Framework (optional) > So What > Action bullets.

**Target length:** 1,500-2,500 words for opinion pieces, 2,500-3,500 for deep dives.

**Quality bar:** 70% publishable. Owen edits for voice and emphasis, not structure.

## Finding what to write next

Filter by `heat: hot` + `status: seed` for the strongest unwritten pieces. Filter by `status: draft-ready` for pieces with first drafts ready for Owen's edit pass. Notes with 3+ adjacent seeds suggest a series.
