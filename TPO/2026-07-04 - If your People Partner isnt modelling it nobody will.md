# If your People Partner isn't modelling it, nobody will

> **The take:** The person responsible for coaching managers on feedback had given zero feedback herself. Second-order signalling kills culture initiatives faster than any policy gap.

## Context (private, not for publication)
Lynnette (Senior People Partner) had given zero Windmill feedback in 30 days while being the person Owen is partnering with to drive the EYS performance framework and manager feedback adoption. Only 2 of ~248 employees had given feedback (Owen and Alessandro). Flagged for the next 1:1 as a modelling issue.

## The insight
Every culture initiative has a moment where it either becomes real or stays a slide deck. That moment isn't the CEO announcement or the all-hands rollout. It's when the people closest to the initiative start behaving differently themselves.

If the People team launches a feedback culture initiative and the People Partners aren't visibly giving feedback, the initiative is dead on arrival. Managers are pattern-matchers. They watch what the people holding them accountable actually do, not what they say to do. When the gap is visible, the message is: "This is theatre. Comply minimally and move on."

The fix is uncomfortable but simple: before you hold anyone accountable for a new standard, make sure your own team is the first and most visible example of it. The People function doesn't get an exemption from the standards it sets. It goes first.

## Evidence
- People Partner: zero feedback given in 30 days, while co-owning the feedback adoption initiative
- Org-wide: only 2 of ~250 employees had given formal feedback in the same period
- Both active feedback-givers were outside the People Partner's direct influence
